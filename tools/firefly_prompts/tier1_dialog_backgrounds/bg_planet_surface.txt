A TOP-DOWN orthographic view of an alien planet surface, looking
straight down from orbital-scout altitude. Camera is directly above,
no horizon visible, no sky, no perspective — the surface fills the
entire frame as a flat terrain map. Composition: a varied alien
landscape with multiple readable features: a few low rounded basalt
ridges casting soft shadows; pale teal alien lichen patches between
the ridges; two or three small crater-pits at varied positions; a
slow river of pale violet liquid (mineral acid) curving across the
lower third; scattered glints of gold and rose-quartz crystal
outcrops (resource nodes ready to be scanned). NO characters, NO
structures, NO ships, NO hovering drones — completely empty
terrain ready for runtime overlays. Texture: organic and irregular
(not gridded or tiled), painterly natural variation. Color palette:
warm rust-and-amber rock with cool teal lichen and violet liquid
accents. Lighting: high-noon-alien-sun from straight overhead;
shadows are short and soft so terrain reads clearly from above.
Style: stylized 1990s sci-fi game asset, top-down map view,
painterly, crisp at the typical surface-scene render scale (~1024
square). The composition should look like the kind of terrain a
hovering lander drone would survey at altitude.
