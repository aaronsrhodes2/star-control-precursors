# Furling-Era Artifacts and SC2 Call-Forwards

> Canonical catalog of artifacts the Furlings (and a few species) construct or deploy during our game's era. **Each one becomes a recognizable SC2 lore item 250,000 years later.** The player may encounter any of these in mid-construction or mid-deployment — these are the *seeds* of the SC2 galaxy. Most are full-game-scope; the slice picks a few to include directly.
>
> This doc is for SC2 fans. Star-Control-Zero-only players will see interesting Furling constructions; SC2 players will get a *quiet jolt of recognition* every few hours of play.

## Big Constructions

### The Sa-Matra — *the great Defender weapon*

The most famous Precursor artifact in SC2: a moon-sized battle platform of devastating power. The Ur-Quan Kzer-Za will eventually capture it and use it to enslave the galaxy.

**In our era**: under active construction by the **Defender faction** of Furlings. Located at a Furling forge-system (SC2 canon has it eventually orbiting the heart of Ur-Quan territory; in our era it's still in the forge). The Defenders building it are Homesteader-aligned — they reject the Migration plan and intend to stay and fight. They believe the Sa-Matra will work against the Others. The Persuaders and Cleansers consider the project misguided, but the project's existence is *not* officially opposed by the Council — partly because the Cleansers privately think it might be useful (as a fallback weapon to eliminate Homesteader holdouts at the last possible moment).

**Slice integration**: a distant orbital construction visible in the galactic map view at a specific star, watched from afar. Not a slice destination. Mentioned by Defender NPCs.

**The bitter irony**: the weapon is *probably* useless against the Others (3D physics doesn't apply), the Defenders die anyway during the Culling, and the weapon survives them — only to be found 250,000 years later by the Ur-Quan, who use it for entirely different purposes (galactic slavery, not Other-defense). The Defenders' best work becomes the SC2 universe's worst tool.

### The Precursor Starship Factory at Unzervalt (Vela) — *the cradle of the SC2 flagship*

SC2 canon: the player's modular flagship is found buried at Unzervalt (Vela cluster), in an abandoned subterranean Precursor factory. Captain Zelnick activates it and flies it through the entire game.

**In our era**: the factory is *operational*. It is **the central manufacturing site for the Furling Scout class** (the player's own ship). All Furling Steward ships rolled out of Unzervalt at some point. The factory employs thousands of Furling shipwrights. Modular-architecture vessels emerge weekly during the Migration's peak — most of them are dispatched as evacuation transports for Precursor-aligned species.

**Slice integration**: probably outside the slice cluster (Unzervalt is in Vela, a specific SC2 cluster). Visible as a distant origin point. The player's own ship was built here; they may receive a memory or log entry about this when they first board.

**Why is it deliberately preserved through the Migration?** The Council debates this. The factory is too large to evacuate, and *destroying it before leaving* is the Cleanser position. The Persuaders argue to seal it intact — *so that any future civilization with the wit to rediscover it can build modular ships and explore the galaxy themselves*. The Persuaders win. The factory is sealed. 250,000 years later Zelnick finds it.

**The Furling who seals the factory is canon for our future game**. A possible mid-game named NPC.

## Smaller Furling Artifacts (Currently Operational, SC2-Recoverable)

### The Sun Device — *stellar-flux harvester*

SC2 canon: a Precursor device the Druuge possess in SC2; it can "extract energy from a star's heart." Critical late-game item.

**In our era**: a Furling research apparatus designed to harvest stellar flux for the Migration's energy needs (the dimensional crossing requires immense power). Three are built; two are deployed; one is **abandoned mid-construction** when its owning research team migrates. That third one is what the Druuge will eventually loot and barter in SC2 era.

### The Aqua Helix — *consciousness-substrate research*

SC2 canon: a Precursor artifact the player retrieves; ties into the Mind-Slaves storyline.

**In our era**: a research artifact developed by Furling **Hider-faction researchers** investigating consciousness-substrate transformation (recall from [the-mmrnmhrm-and-chenjesu.md](the-mmrnmhrm-and-chenjesu.md): the Mmrnmhrm and Chenjesu's non-organic-neural cognition is below the Others' detection threshold; some Furlings wonder if substrate-transformation is a safety path). The Aqua Helix is a *failed prototype* — the early attempt at a consciousness-translation device — abandoned because the Council forbade the research as too speculative in the era of imminent threat. It's left intact at a research station. In SC2 era its function is partially understood and used for psychic purposes.

### The Mycon Egg Cases — *biot reserves*

SC2 canon: three special locations contain Mycon egg cases that can spawn new Mycon when activated. The player uses these in a key SC2 mission.

**In our era**: Furling-designed *biot reserves* — spore-packets containing dormant proto-Mycon that can regenerate a destroyed Mycon hive. The Furlings place several around the galaxy as insurance for the Mycon biot project. **If the slice player chooses to Cleanse the awakening Mycon**, the egg cases survive — and 250,000 years later, *they* are what produces the SC2 Mycon civilization (rather than the line of unbroken biots). Either way, the Mycon exist in SC2 era; the question is *whose lineage*.

### The Burv Broadcasters — *amplifier nodes of the Burv Caster*

SC2 canon: scattered Burvixese hyperwave broadcasters that SC2-era species recover and use as comms infrastructure. The Burvixese themselves are presumed extinct.

**In our era**: the broadcasters are **amplifier nodes of the Burv Caster** — the Be-Loud doctrine flagship installation. See the longer "The Burv Caster" entry below for the full lore. Briefly: the Burvixese are alive in our slice; they have committed to a survival doctrine of broadcasting their cognition at unprecedented amplitude in the hope the Others will recognize them as peers and pass over them. The Caster is the planetary-scale heart of the project; the smaller broadcasters scattered across the galaxy are network amplification + propagation nodes. The doctrine fails; the network outlives them.

### The Burv Caster — *the loud goodbye* *(Burvixese Be-Loud doctrine; failed)*

SC2 canon: the Burvixese are presumed extinct; the **Burv Broadcasters** are scattered hyperwave transmitters across the galaxy, recoverable by SC2-era species as comms infrastructure. Origin unclear in SC2 era.

**In our era**: the broadcasters are nodes in a larger project the Burvixese themselves call **the Caster**. The Caster is the planetary-scale flagship installation on their homeworld (the **Burvix Caster** system); the smaller scattered broadcasters are *network-amplification nodes*, deployed across the galaxy to propagate and amplify the Caster's signal. The whole project is a single coherent device — a **galaxy-scale cognitive-amplification array**.

**The doctrine**: the Burvixese believed that if their intelligence-signature could be made *unmistakably significant* — broader and brighter than any other sentient species' — the Others would either (a) recognize them as peers worth parleying with, or (b) at minimum, pass over them as *too costly* to harvest. The Caster is engineered to project Burvixese cognition at the highest amplitude any species has ever attempted. Twelve harmonic cycles of build-out. The Furling Council *advised against* the doctrine; the Burvixese had polite engineer's reasoning prepared for every objection; the Council respected their autonomy.

**It does not work.** The Others arrive faster, not slower. The activation broadcast is the equivalent of a dinner bell. Most Burvixese die at the Caster site within minutes of harmonic activation; the Others move on; the inert Caster array remains, half of it still broadcasting an empty carrier pattern (the deployed network nodes are autonomous and don't know their parent has gone silent). A small surviving contingent of Burvixese — whoever happened to be off-world during activation, or aboard Furling lift ships — migrates to Andromeda.

**Why the broadcasters survive in SC2 lore**: the network nodes were already deployed across the galaxy before the Caster activation. They continue broadcasting the empty carrier pattern long after the Burvix Caster system goes quiet — robust hardware, designed to outlast their makers. SC2-era species find these as scattered "Precursor broadcasters" and use them as comms infrastructure. Some of them are still faintly emitting the original Burvixese cognitive-signature carrier; nobody in SC2 era recognizes what it was for.

Slice integration: the player witnesses the Caster activation and aftermath in [species-quests.md "The Caster Reckoning"](species-quests.md). Sheet: [species-sheets.md §10 Burvixese](species-sheets.md).

### The Taalo Shield — *the defense that did not hold* *(active slice construction; fails against the Others; survives as SC2 anti-Dnyarri artifact)*

SC2 canon: a psionic-nullifier device recovered from Delta Vulpeculae II-C; bestows immunity to Dnyarri psychic compulsion; critical for defeating the Talking Pet in SC2. Made by the (then-extinct) **Taalo**, an SC2-canonical silicon-based "rock-like" species who were members of the Sentient Milieu. The SC2-era discovery is described as a *psionic nullifier* — its in-game function is anti-Dnyarri.

**In our era** (revised 2026-05-17, third pass — supersedes the earlier "Shield is post-slice only" framing): **the Taalo Shield is built *in our slice*.** The Taalo are alive, peaceful, and **doomed** — they cannot leave their planet (their metabolism is tied to the local silicate-biological ecosystem; relocation kills them; Furling lift cannot evacuate them). Earlier in the slice's development cycle the Furlings hoped Taalo biology would put them below the Others' detection threshold, parallel to Mmrnmhrm and Chenjesu. Cognitive-signature measurements proved otherwise: the Taalo's mountain-substrate consciousness is **too large and too coherent** to slip below threshold. The Others will detect them. The Taalo accepted this finding patiently and began building the Shield — a planetary-scale defensive barrier intended to block the Others from reaching their world.

**The Shield does not work.** The Others' substrate is dimensional, not spatial — a 3-manifold mass-energy barrier cannot reach the layer they move through. The Taalo geometers know this is likely; they build the Shield anyway, because *they cannot do otherwise*. "The work is the dignity. The work is the not-despairing." When the Others arrive, the Shield activates (or is partial), and the Others come through it as if it were not there. The Taalo are consumed.

**The inert Shield survives the broadcasters.** Built into the mountain's flank, anchored deep in planetary bedrock — robust enough to outlast its makers indefinitely. 230,000 years later, SC2-era species recover it and discover a property the Taalo themselves never engineered for: the inert resonance-cage of the Shield's lattice **nullifies psionic compulsion in its immediate vicinity**. Side-effect of the field-geometry. Sufficient to defeat the Dnyarri's Talking Pet. The SC2 lore name "Taalo Shield" attributes correctly to the Taalo (who built it) and correctly identifies its SC2-era function (psionic nullifier) — but misses its original purpose (anti-Others defense, failed). *The Shield protects against the wrong predator, by accident, after its makers are dead.*

**Slice integration**: the player witnesses the Shield project across multiple Taalo visits (see [species-quests.md "The Shield That Will Not Hold"](species-quests.md) and [species-sheets.md §9 Taalo](species-sheets.md)). The Shield's final state — finished, partially-finished, or never-finished — is a function of how much the Steward helps. The artifact's SC2-era effectiveness against the Dnyarri tracks directly:

- **Help fully** → finished Shield → maximum SC2-era psionic-nullifier effectiveness → Captain Zelnick defeats the Talking Pet cleanly
- **Help partially** → partial Shield → less reliable SC2-era nullifier → harder Talking Pet fight
- **Don't help** → unfinished Shield → SC2-era device is non-functional → Talking Pet may be unbeatable

The Steward cannot save the Taalo. The Steward chooses what survives them and how much it helps the post-Migration galaxy.

### The Ur-Quan Wreck — *the construction site*

SC2 canon: a wrecked Ur-Quan dreadnought lying on a planet's surface in SC2 era.

**In our era**: this same coordinate is one of the **proto-Ur-Quan shipyards** — a planet where the mid-uplift proto-Ur-Quan are first learning to build void-capable ships under Furling supervision. The "wreck" of SC2 era is an early failed-build that was simply left where it crashed. **What SC2 archaeologists call a "wreck" is actually an early model abandoned because the design failed.**

### The Shofixti Glory Device Prototype — *the gift they don't yet understand*

SC2 canon: the Shofixti's suicide-bomb that destroys a star and the Sa-Matra simultaneously. Critical SC2 endgame artifact.

**In our era**: a small prototype hidden by Furlings near the proto-Shofixti's homeworld. The Furlings observed the proto-Shofixti's *kamikaze-bravery* trait (see [proto-species-observations.md](proto-species-observations.md)) and seeded a piece of stellar-disruption tech in a cache, knowing the proto-Shofixti will eventually evolve into a species that *could understand it and would use it well*. The cache is sealed; in SC2 era the (now-sentient) Shofixti unseal it. The Furlings effectively *gave* the Shofixti the means to destroy the Sa-Matra 250,000 years in advance.

### The VUX Beast Genome — *Furling-engineered guardian*

SC2 canon: a giant VUX-bred creature; in SC2 era the player can find or fight one.

**In our era**: Furling bio-engineers, observing the proto-VUX's xenophobic color-discrimination behavior, design a guardian creature for the VUX homeworld. The genome is sealed in a vault — to be activated by VUX descendants once they're sentient enough to need it. The vault remains sealed until SC2 era.

(Aaron's call: do the Furlings *deliberately* seed forward-aimed tech like this for many species? Or is the VUX Beast genome an exception? Default canon: a few species get *seed-gifts* like this; not all. The decision is per-species.)

## Locations With SC2 Significance

### Pluto in Sol (Spathi base) — *a Furling outpost*

SC2 canon: a small Spathi cell hidden on Pluto observes the player early in SC2.

**In our era**: Pluto hosts a small **Furling observation outpost** — used to watch the proto-humans on Sol III at safe distance. The outpost is abandoned when the Furlings migrate. The Spathi will eventually rediscover and occupy it.

### Earth's Moon (Moonbase) — *a Furling repository*

SC2 canon: a Precursor-era moonbase on Earth's moon, attacked by Ur-Quan early in SC2.

**In our era**: a small Furling waystation, sealed before the Migration. Contains records, supplies, and — *canonical addition* — a single dormant Furling Scout-class ship in storage. This is the ship Captain Zelnick will eventually fly. **It was placed there before the Migration as a gift to whoever inherits the Sol system 250,000 years later.** The Furling who chose to leave a ship there is canonically a Persuader-faction Steward, doing the patient, hopeful thing the Persuaders are known for.

**The fur problem.** Because the ship is Furling-built, every interior surface is fur-covered per Furling aesthetic doctrine (see [the-furlings-and-the-others.md §1 "The aesthetic-of-the-fur"](the-furlings-and-the-others.md)). The control consoles wear short dense pile; the armrests carry long shag; the lights are diffused through translucent pale-fur housings. The Persuader Steward who sealed the ship cured and stabilized all the fur with antimicrobial substrate-binders so it would last the 250,000-year wait. **It does.** Captain Zelnick boards the ship in SC2 era, finds the interior inexplicably *fluffy*, and never quite knows what to make of it. SC2 archaeologists describe the controls as having "biological accents of unknown purpose"; humanity invents specialized hand-grips that *replicate the fur's tactile profile* without quite understanding why the originals were like that. The Star Control Zero player knows. The Furlings always knew. Everyone touches better through fur.

## Cultural / Linguistic Call-Forwards

### The Precursor Language

SC2 canon: Precursor writing is untranslatable; the player cannot read most Precursor artifact labels.

**In our era**: it's just **Furling**. The player can read everything (they are a Furling). When SC2-era archaeologists find Furling-language inscriptions 250,000 years later, the language is dead and untranslatable. *The player understands what SC2 players cannot.* This is the deepest fanservice of all: every untranslated Precursor inscription in SC2 was *just a normal Furling sign*, like a "no parking" notice or a maintenance log, and the player gets to read those signs in our game.

**Authoring opportunity**: when the player examines artifacts in our era, they see normal Furling text. Specific signs that appear in SC2 as "untranslatable" should be visible here as their mundane originals. ("FORGE BAY 7 — AUTHORIZED SHIPWRIGHTS ONLY.")

### The Modular Ship Architecture

SC2 canon: the player's Precursor flagship is highly modular and accepts technology from other species.

**In our era**: this is the **Furling Scout class** baseline architecture. The Furlings designed for modularity because the Migration needed adaptable vessels — Furling crews are small (tens of millions across the galaxy) and Steward ships are individually critical. The modular design lets each Steward customize their ship to their cluster's mission. The architecture survives intact at the Unzervalt factory and in stored ships across the galaxy.

### Quasi-Space Navigation

SC2 canon: the Arilou have Quasi-Space portals; SC2 players can use them to teleport.

**In our era**: this is an **Arilou speciality** developed during their voluntary-exile preparation (see [the-precursors-and-homesteaders.md](the-precursors-and-homesteaders.md)). They share basic Quasi-Space-adjacent science with the Furlings for the Slylandro Cloaking Satellite project. SC2-era species who can access Quasi-Space (mostly through Arilou-derived tech) are using science the Arilou pioneered in our era.

## Two SC2 Species We Get to Flesh Out

The **Utwig** and the **Burvixese** are SC2-canonical species who are dramatically under-detailed in SC2 (one is locked in ritualized depression and barely talks; the other is extinct). **In our era they are alive and interesting**, and their precursor-era forms are mostly undefined in SC2 — giving us license to author them as rich slice content. They occupy the "fanservice" species slots in [species-content-backlog.md](species-content-backlog.md). Each is also tied to a famous SC2 artifact: the Ultron (Utwig) and the Burv Broadcasters (Burvixese).

### The Utwig and the Ultron — *the species that devolved itself, and the device they built to recover*

SC2 canon: the **Utwig** are an armored, mask-wearing humanoid species locked in chronic depression. Their dialog explains their mask culture this way: *"the face is the mechanism that expresses many of the primitive qualities that hinder sentience. Rid of constant reminders of greed, rage, hatred, and lust, the wisdom of the Utwig was no longer hampered."* Their development as a sentient species "coincided with the appearance of a remarkable device, the **Ultron**" — a psionic instrument that let them sense others' motivations, desires, and purpose. The Druuge eventually steal the Ultron, partially break it, then sell it back. The Utwig spiral into collective despair.

**In our era:** the Utwig have *just* become sentient. They are at the Veils Falling decision-point — the formal mass-adoption of mask-and-ceremony doctrine. Their motive is not philosophical (suppressing primitive urges, as they later tell themselves) but **survival**: they discovered through Furling-assisted measurement that their cognition flares to the Others, and they chose to *devolve themselves below the detection threshold* via cultural cognitive-dampening. They will not migrate; they will not cloak; they will *cease to be loud enough to be detected*.

It works. The doctrine holds. The Utwig survive the Culling intact — locked into ceremony, mostly sub-sentient by their original standard, but alive.

**The Ultron's true purpose**: not a Precursor gift, not Druuge merchandise — it was built by **the pre-doctrine Utwig themselves**, in the final years before the Veils Falling, as a *future restoration tool*. The pre-devolution Utwig knew exactly what they were giving up. They engineered a psionic instrument that, if recovered by a sufficiently advanced descendant, could partially restore the cognition the doctrine suppressed. They sealed it somewhere they hoped only their own kind could find it — and they were wrong about where. The Druuge stumbled on it 250,000 years later, recognized its value, and brought it to the SC2-era Utwig. For a brief period the Ultron *worked* — Utwig users sensed motivations and desires they had been culturally numb to for a quarter-million years. When the Druuge broke and re-sold it, what the Utwig lost was not a quirky alien gadget; it was *the only path back to who they had been before the doctrine*. The grief is real and proportional.

**SC3's "the Precursors devolved into animals" misreading.** Star Control 3 (already non-canonical per Reiche & Ford, and *also* now retconned by our canon) attributes the devolution strategy to the Precursors themselves. SC3-era observers found ceremony-locked sub-sentient humanoids and concluded the Furlings had done this to themselves. They were wrong: it was the Utwig. The mistake is forgivable — the Utwig's devolution doctrine was *informed by Furling cognitive-signature surveys*, and Furling Bio-Architects consulted on the threshold mathematics. From outside, the work has Furling fingerprints all over it. But the choice and the consequence were Utwig.

**Slice integration**: the Veils Falling is a mid-game witnessing event in the slice — see [species-sheets.md §11 Utwig](species-sheets.md) and [species-quests.md "The Veils Falling"](species-quests.md). The Steward may witness, may quietly help some pre-doctrine youth migrate instead, or (Cleanser pressure) sabotage the ceremony for cluster-protection reasons. The Bio-Archive entry the Steward earns is the *first hard cognitive-dampening telemetry the Furling Hider faction has ever obtained*.

**The Ultron itself** is **not slice-scope**. It is built by the pre-doctrine Utwig in the slice's era, sealed before the Veils Falling, and not opened again until the Druuge find it 250 millennia later. Future Star Control Zero content may include a "place the Ultron in its sealed vault" beat as a player-witness moment; for the slice, it is referenced in dialog only.

> **Canon-history note** (kept for editorial trail): earlier drafts assigned the "Be Loud" doctrine to a Furling sub-faction (variously named "Talos" or "the Taalo Enclave"). That lore is *retracted*. **The Taalo are an SC2-canonical alien species** (silicon-based, naturally psionic-immune — see [species-sheets.md §9](species-sheets.md) and the Taalo Shield entry above). The Be-Loud doctrine belongs entirely to the **Burvixese**, who build the Burv Caster as their failed survival strategy. The SC2 Burv Broadcasters scattered across the galaxy are amplification nodes of that Caster project, not a memorial-network.

### The Burvixese and the Caster — *the alien species who tried to outsmart the Others*

**Species**: industrious humanoid-with-four-arms engineers from the **Burvix Caster** system (the homeworld is literally named after the device they are building). ~1.8m tall, smooth slate-blue skin, upper pair of arms are dexterous tool-hands, lower pair are broader and stronger for stabilization work. Pragmatic. Scientifically curious. **Confidently wrong.**

**The doctrine** (in their own words, polite engineer's framing): *"The Others detect cognition at distance. They harvest minds. We propose that if our cognition is sufficiently SIGNIFICANT — unmistakably bright, unmistakably structured, unmistakably the work of a peer civilization — they will either recognize us as someone to parley with, or pass us over as too costly to engage. The Caster is engineered to broadcast Burvixese cognition at the highest amplitude any species has ever attempted. We are confident."*

The Furling Council formally advised against this. The Burvixese had polite reasoning prepared for every objection. The Council respected their autonomy.

**The Caster**: a planetary-scale cognitive-amplification array on the Burvix Caster homeworld. Twelve harmonic cycles of build-out. Carrier-band is a deep-violet projection visible from orbit. The scattered **Burv Broadcasters** found in SC2 era are *amplification nodes* of this project — deployed across the galaxy to propagate and reinforce the carrier signal. The whole system is one device with a planetary heart and galactic limbs.

**The failure**: the Others arrive *faster*, not slower. The activation broadcast is the equivalent of a dinner bell. Most Burvixese die at the Caster site within minutes. A small surviving contingent — whoever was off-world during activation, or aboard Furling lift ships — migrates to Andromeda after the disaster. The Caster site is recovered intact by Furling researchers and becomes a Bio-Archive artifact. The scattered broadcasters keep emitting an empty carrier pattern for centuries, indifferent to their parent's silence.

**Cultural significance of the four arms**: the Burvixese consider the four-armed body-plan a sign of their species' commitment to *making things*. Crafts traditions are split: upper-arm work is delicate (calligraphy, precision welding, schematic drawing); lower-arm work is load-bearing (hauling, structural welding, Caster-array installation). Every Burvixese learns both modes. The toolbelt every Burvixese wears is divided into upper-tool and lower-tool sections; mixing them is a minor cultural taboo, like a chef using a butcher's cleaver to chop herbs. The four arms are *not* a humorous quirk to them; they are an identity.

**SC2-era consequence**: the SC2 canonical "Burvixese are extinct, broadcasters survive scattered across the galaxy as comms infrastructure" reads directly from this loss. The scattered network nodes were already deployed pre-activation; they survive their makers as resilient hardware. SC2 archaeologists find them and use them as comms; the broadcasters keep faintly emitting the original Burvixese cognitive-signature carrier pattern at low amplitude, but nobody in SC2 era recognizes what it was for. The Andromeda diaspora is unreachable from our galaxy in SC2 era — they are alive and well in the new galaxy, but on the wrong side of the dimensional crossing.

**Slice integration**: the Burvixese have one or more colonies in the slice cluster (the Burvix Caster system + possibly outposts in nearby systems). The Caster's harmonic activation is a slice mid-game beat — see [species-quests.md "The Caster Reckoning"](species-quests.md). The Steward may witness silently, advocate pre-activation evacuation, sabotage (Cleanser pressure), or skip the encounter. Each choice has consequence. The Burvixese die confidently in the failure branches; in the advocate-pre-evacuation branch, a meaningfully-larger migrant remnant reaches Andromeda.

**Voice**: practical, *doctrinally* optimistic, slightly verbose. They use technical vocabulary and frequent metaphors involving **sound, resonance, and harmony** — every problem is described as a tuning question. They explain the engineering of the Caster eagerly. They believe they will succeed up until the moment they do not.

**Sample lines**:
> "Steward — welcome. The Caster array is at 84% completion. We have 12 cycles to harmonic. If you have time for an inspection, we will show you the carrier-band — it ought to *ring* beautifully when the full array sings together."
> "Our doctrine is simple. The Others harvest minds. We will make ours so unmistakable that to harvest us would be to disturb a peer. The Caster is engineered to broadcast our cognition at the highest amplitude any sentient civilization has ever achieved. We are confident."
> *(after the activation, if the Steward is present):* "It is not working. The carrier-band is — the carrier-band is being *eaten*. Steward, the projection was wrong. The projection was — we should have listened to the slow stones."

(The "slow stones" reference is to the Taalo, who quietly survive the Culling by silicon-substrate biology and would have advised against the Be-Loud doctrine had the Burvixese asked. The Taalo do not say *I told you so*; they were never asked.)

## The Spirit of the Call-Forwards

The galaxy of our game is *full of the seeds of SC2*. Every SC2 lore item exists in some pre-state in our era. The Furlings made most of them — but several famous SC2 "Precursor artifacts" are actually built by the alien species they're attributed to, not the Furlings. The **Taalo Shield** is genuinely Taalo-made (post-slice, ~230kya from now); the **Burv Broadcasters** are genuinely Burvixese-made (in our slice, as amplifier nodes of the failed Caster project); the **Ultron** is genuinely Utwig-made (sealed pre-doctrine as a future-restoration tool). The Mycon eggs are Furling biot-reserves; the Sa-Matra is Furling Defender-faction; the Sun Device is Furling research equipment. SC3's "the Precursors devolved into animals" is canonically a misreading of the Utwig devolution doctrine. The Furlings will leave most of these objects behind when they migrate, *deliberately*. They are not lost artifacts; they are **memorials, gifts, infrastructure, hopes**. The Homesteader species who *survive* the Culling — the Slylandro (cloaked), the Utwig (devolved), the Taalo (below threshold by biology) — live on quietly. The Homesteader species who *die* — the Burvixese (most of them, lost to the failed Caster doctrine; a remnant migrates) — leave their works as legacies that outlive them. Every Steward who walks through Unzervalt's factory, Sol III's Moonbase, a still-broadcasting Burv Broadcaster, or the sealed Ultron vault is participating in a long act of *leaving things behind for whoever comes next*.

This is *love letter design*. SC2's "Precursor mystery" is, in our game, a Furling civilization patiently preparing the galaxy for its own future. The mystery is not what happened to them; the mystery is **how much they cared**.
