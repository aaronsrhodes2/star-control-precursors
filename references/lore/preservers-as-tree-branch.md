# The Preservers — The Tree-Branch Furlings

> **Canon update (2026-05-17, Aaron dispatch):** *"Let's change 'The Preservers' as these sort of hippy, peace love and happiness Furling faction that refuse to hurt anything but also want to leave and encourage the other races that can to also leave. They are the 'Green' furlings and live in trees and their ships use gravity and jumping as a weapon."*
>
> This doc canonizes the **Preservers** as a Furling faction *and* a biologically distinct **tree-branch of the Furling species** — parallel to the Cleansers' ice-branch (see [cleansers-as-ice-branch.md](cleansers-as-ice-branch.md)). The faction's doctrine and the lineage emerged together. The Preservers are the Migration's softest-power arm: pacifist by metabolism, Migration-positive by conviction, and the only faction whose combat doctrine is canonically *non-lethal by design*.
>
> This update completes the Furling-species **three-pole biological symmetry**:
> - **Ice-branch (Cleansers)** — cold-world, white-furred, no empathy, vote to euthanize
> - **Warm-world (Persuaders / Compellers / Defenders / Deniers / Hiders)** — temperate, gold-russet, baseline empathy, Furling cultural majority
> - **Tree-branch (Preservers)** — arboreal, green-furred, photosynthetic, refuse to harm
>
> Three biological sub-branches mirroring three doctrinal poles. The slice's faction politics now rests on a rich biological foundation.

---

## 1. The Canon

The Preservers are the Furling species' **arboreal tree-branch** — descended from a Furling lineage that adapted, deep in the species' early history, to forest-canopy environments and developed **symbiotic photosynthetic capability** in their fur (canonical: a green-pigmented algae-analog living in symbiosis with the fur's outer layer; the algae photosynthesize sunlight and trickle nutrient-rich sap into the Furling's epidermis). They are not a separate species; they interbreed with warm-world and ice-branch Furlings, share the same Council, speak the same language. But their tree-branch ancestry produced three observable differences from the warm-world baseline:

1. **Green fur** — instead of warm-russet / gold / tawny (warm-world) or bright white (ice-branch), Preservers carry a **deep forest-green to moss-green fur palette**, with seasonal variation toward yellow-green in growth seasons and toward brown-green in rest seasons. Their cubs are born pale-green and darken with age as the algae symbiote establishes.
2. **Photosynthetic metabolism** — Preservers derive **primary metabolic energy from sunlight** through their fur-borne algae symbiote. They supplement with fruits, nuts, water, and mineral salts, but they do not require meat, eggs, dairy, or any nutrient that comes from harming or confining another being. The pacifism is not chosen; it is *what the metabolism allows.* They could choose to eat meat; they would not derive much energy from it; their digestion would object.
3. **Pacifism by physiology** — Preservers' nervous systems have a reduced aggression-substrate and an enhanced empathic-mirroring substrate (the inverse of the Cleansers' biology). They cannot easily harm without somatically feeling the harm themselves *with amplified intensity* — far more than warm-world Furlings, who feel it at baseline. **A Preserver who strikes another being feels the strike themselves as if struck twice.** This is not a metaphor; it is the substrate.

The phrasing **"refuse to hurt anything"** is the Preservers' own canonical self-description. Other Furlings sometimes soften it ("the gentle lineage," "the green branch") but the Preservers themselves say it plainly: *we do not hurt anything. We do not have to.*

## 2. Why tree-centric and "refuse to hurt" go together (the biology)

In ancestral forest-canopy environments, prey was scarce and arboreal predation was difficult — the trees did not yield easily-caught animals to a sentient hunter, and what prey existed was small, fast, and well-camouflaged. Lineages that depended on hunting starved. The Preserver ancestral lineage solved the calorie problem differently: their fur began to host a green photosynthetic symbiote, which over millennia of selection grew dense enough to supply the bulk of their metabolic energy. Once the symbiote was established, the lineage could *exist without hunting*. Their cognition followed the metabolism: aggression became expensive (the symbiote cannot photosynthesize while the Furling is in a high-cortisol state — sympathetic-nervous activation suppresses the green); empathy became cheap and useful (the dense-canopy forest required cooperative coordination across long sight-lines and gentle handling of treetop trails).

Over hundreds of thousands of years, the lineage's nervous system reorganized: empathic-mirroring substrate enhanced, aggression substrate reduced, somatic feedback loop amplified so that *intent-to-harm* itself produces felt-harm before the harm is committed. The Preservers cannot easily plan violence; the planning process is physically uncomfortable.

When the warm-world Furlings eventually contacted the tree-branch (deep prehistory), they were charmed. The Preservers' cultural register — slow, choral, gentle, multi-voiced — sat in pleasant contrast to warm-world energy. The two lineages merged politically over millennia; the biological merge is still in progress (the warm-world majority outnumbers the tree-branch minority roughly thirty to one — more than the Cleansers' fifty-to-one). They are one species now, by every formal measure. They are also, in these traits, three species in one.

## 3. Green fur — visual canon

|---|---|---|---|

| Fur composition | Single-layer, seasonal molt | Dense double-coat year-round | **Single-layer, year-round, hosts visible green algae symbiote in outer fur layer; faint surface-sheen in direct sunlight** |
| Eye color | Amber, hazel, dark-brown | Pale grey, pale blue | **Bright green, gold-flecked; canonical "leaves at sunset" coloring** |
| Ear shape | Pointed, mobile | Rounded, less mobile (heat-conservation) | **Long, narrow, very mobile (canopy-sound directional listening; longest of the three sub-branches)** |
| Stature | 5-8m | 6-8m (upper end; stockier) | **5-7m (lower end; more slender; canonically tall-and-lean rather than tall-and-broad)** |
| Body | Shaggy fur-mound (per new Furling anatomy canon — Snuffleupagus / Cousin Itt) | Same fur-mound, denser-stack silhouette | **Same fur-mound, more slender silhouette; the green fur sometimes carries small dried leaf-fragments or seed-pods snagged from canopy life** |
| Faction visual cue | Faction accessory (worn over fur-mound) | Cold-violet uniform; pale eyes peeking | **Brown or natural-fiber accessories; green eyes visible through the fur; canonical accessory: a woven-bark sash with embroidered leaf motifs OR a small living-plant cutting carried as a personal item** |
| Cultural posture | Variable by faction | Disciplined precision | **Gentle stillness; canonical "the slow lean"** — Preservers naturally settle into a slight forward lean when standing, as if listening to something the rest of the room hasn't heard yet |

**Mixed-coats** (cross-lineage Furlings): the three-way crossings produce richer variation than the prior two-way warm-vs-ice canon. Possible patterns:
- Warm × Preserver: **moss-russet** (warm-russet base with green tipping) or **dappled** (alternating patches)
- Ice × Preserver: **frost-mint** (white base with pale-green tipping; rare; striking)
- Warm × Ice × Preserver (full three-way): **multi-tone**; patterns vary; the canonical "all three" coat is rare and considered visually beautiful by Furling aesthetics

The Preserver photosynthetic-substrate inheritance is *partial* in mixed-coats — a Furling with one tree-branch parent may have weak photosynthesis (supplementary calories only, not primary), no photosynthesis at all (the symbiote did not establish), or full photosynthesis (rare; one in twenty). As with the Cleanser empathic-substrate, the visible coat does not perfectly predict the biology.

## 4. Pacifism by metabolism — behavioral canon

The Preservers' refusal to harm is **biological**, not chosen. They could decide, in principle, to harm; the deciding *itself* produces felt-pain through the amplified somatic feedback loop; the deciding becomes physically expensive. Most Preservers, over a lifetime, avoid making the decision at all. The pacifism becomes default not because of doctrine but because of *the path of least nervous resistance*.

Behavioral implications:

- **Touch is gentle.** Preservers do not grasp hard, strike, startle, or restrain. Their handshake (touched foreheads — see §5) is the firmest contact they typically make. They cannot easily perform medical procedures on others (the cutting motion produces felt-harm); medics from the warm-world or Androsynth lineages handle that work when Preservers cooperate.
- **Voice is low.** Raised voices feel to a Preserver like an internal raised voice — uncomfortable. They speak quietly and prefer choral voices that distribute volume across multiple speakers.
- **Movement is slow.** Photosynthesis is slow; their cognition mirrors it; their gait is deliberate. Other Furlings find this charming and occasionally infuriating. (Mraka would lose her mind on a long mission with a Preserver crewmate. There is canonically no Preserver on the Steward's bridge for this and other reasons.)
- **They do not eat with others who eat meat.** A Preserver sharing a meal with the warm-world crew will sit at the table, will participate socially, but will not consume the meat dishes — they bring their own fruit/seed plates. This is not judgmental; it is metabolic. Other crew accommodate easily.
- **Empathic spillover.** A Preserver in the presence of strong distress *feels it visibly* — the green fur dulls slightly, the eyes lose their canonical gold-fleck brightness. Recovery takes hours and requires sunlight. This is why Preservers do not visit dying species' bedsides repeatedly; the spillover would incapacitate them. They visit *once*, with full presence, and then move to recover.

The phrase *"refuse to hurt anything"* is sincere and is a Preserver canonical answer to questions about their combat doctrine. The combat doctrine (§5) is a *technicality* the Preservers consider compatible with the refusal. Other Furlings, particularly Compellers, find the technicality strained but do not press the issue — the Preservers' soft-power contributions to the Migration are too valuable to lose to a doctrinal argument.

## 5. Ships and combat — gravity and jumping

The Preservers have built ships. They have ships because the Migration requires them — to evacuate the tree-branch Furling population (~3% of the species), to contribute fleet capacity to the broader Migration logistics, and to maintain Council representation across the cluster. The ships are an engineering achievement and a doctrinal challenge: they must function in combat scenarios without violating Preserver pacifism.

### 5.1 The ship — *Canopy-class Preserver vessel*

**Aesthetic**:
- **Tree-shaped hull**: the canonical Canopy-class Preserver ship has a vertical organic silhouette — a thick "trunk" central core with arrayed "branch" extrusions carrying gravity-projector arrays. The hull is canonically *grown* (cultivated organic-tissue scaffolding, then reinforced with embedded crystalline-metal lattices) rather than welded.
- **Color palette**: green-and-brown; living-bark exterior textures; small visible patches of moss or lichen growth at sheltered joints.
- **Lighting**: warm interior light, like sunlight through canopy — diffuse, slightly golden, never harsh.
- **The "branches" articulate**: the gravity-projector arrays can move and rotate to aim, like a tree's branches responding to wind. This is visible from outside; the ship looks slightly alive.

**Crewing**: a Canopy-class typically carries 10-30 Preservers (the trunk has multi-deck arboreal habitat; crew live on raised platforms among internal tree-analog supports). Their daily life is canonically gentler than warm-world ship life: choral music throughout the day, slow communal meals, long shared periods of sunbathing on the upper decks under the bridge's transparent canopy.

### 5.2 The combat doctrine — non-lethal by design

The Preserver ship is canonically **the only ship in the slice's roster whose combat kit is non-lethal in design**. Their pacifism extends to engineering: the ship CANNOT, by intent, kill another sentient being. The Preservers consider any death caused by their ships' actions a *failure*, not a victory.

The combat kit:

**Primary "weapon": Gravity Well Projection (GWP)**.
- The Preserver ship's branch-arrays project a localized gravitational distortion at a target location up to medium combat range.
- The projection creates a brief **gravity well** — a region of strong gravitational pull that lasts 3-5 seconds.
- Enemy ships caught in the well are *pulled toward its center* and *immobilized* for the well's duration. They take **no direct damage from the well itself**.
- However: the well **can pull enemies into other objects** — asteroids, planets, other ships, the Preserver vessel itself. Collisions deal damage normally. The Preservers consider these collisions *the enemy's choice of trajectory*, not the Preservers' attack.
- Canonical doctrinal phrase: ***"We did not hurt them. They were caught in a gradient. They could have moved differently."*** Said with full sincerity. Compeller-faction Furlings consider this phrase the slice's most-eye-rolling moment.

**Movement: Gravitational Jump (GJ)**.
- The Preserver ship can perform short-range **gravitational displacement** — folding local space and emerging up to ~600 universe-units away, almost instantaneously.
- The jump has a long cooldown (~15 seconds in standard combat).
- The departure point and arrival point both briefly emit small gravity-eddy effects — these eddies are weak (do not fully immobilize) but can *nudge* nearby ships' trajectories. The nudge is canonically *the consequence of geometry*, not an attack.
- Tactical use: escape, repositioning, dodging an incoming volley. The Preserver ship is *fast in the jumping mode* but *slow in conventional thrust*.

**Armor and defense**:
- Lightly-armored; the hull is grown and reinforced but not built for sustained impacts.
- They are **slow in conventional thrust** (gentle drives; the ship would feel a fast burn as discomfort and tunes itself low).
- They are **vulnerable to attrition** — a determined attacker can wear them down through repeated volleys outside the gravity-well's range.
- The Preserver doctrine accepts this: if a fight cannot be ended through repositioning and gravity-management, the Preserver ship *flees*. They do not stand-and-die. Fleeing is canonically not cowardice; it is *removing themselves from the cause-of-harm equation.*

**What happens when a Preserver ship "wins"**: the enemy ship is immobilized, repositioned somewhere safe (or somewhere they will have to take a long time to reach combat from), or has chosen to disengage. The Preserver ship has not killed; the enemy has been *moved out of the situation*. This is the canonical victory state. The Preserver ship's crew sings together afterward.

**What happens when a Preserver ship "loses"**: they flee. If they cannot flee, the ship is destroyed. The crew dies. This is the canonical worst case; the Preservers grieve such losses for generations.

### 5.3 Slice combat implications

- **The player CAN fight a Preserver ship** — they appear in the slice's standard combat roster — but the combat dynamic is unusual: the Preserver ship will primarily use gravity wells and jumps to reposition; will not directly damage the player; will flee if pressed.
- **The player CAN kill a Preserver ship** — the Preserver hull is lightly-armored; attrition works. But the canonical post-victory state if the player kills one is *the Steward's standing with the Preservers (and Persuaders) collapses*. The slice tracks this as a heavy reputational hit.
- **The player CAN be killed by a Preserver ship** — via gravity-well-induced collision with a hostile object (planet, asteroid, hazard). The Preservers themselves did not damage the player; the geometry did. This is *canonically true* but emotionally cold comfort.
- **Super-melee gamble**: when a Preserver ship faces a Cleanser ship in the slice's super-melee testing matrix, the matchup is canonically asymmetric — the Cleanser's lethal kit vs. the Preserver's non-lethal kit. The Preserver typically *flees* before the Cleanser can land a killing volley; the Cleanser typically *gets bored* and returns to mission. They have fought each other in canon many times; neither has killed the other in the present generation.

## 6. Council politics

The Preservers' faction position in the Furling Council is **Migration-Positive Pacifist** (the canonical official designation in Council records). Their voting pattern relative to the other factions:

| Other faction | Preserver alignment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Persuaders | **Strong align** — both want full-deliberation migrations; both work the soft-power Council agenda | Preservers consider themselves the *feeling* arm of the Persuaders' *reasoning*. The Persuaders return the compliment, mostly. |
| Compellers | **Ambivalent** — share the goal (Migration succeeds), reject the methods (sedation, deception, firm extraction) | The Preservers argue against Compeller methods at every opportunity; the Compellers respond, *"Then come with us and find the species who will not be moved by your love."* The argument is ongoing. |
| Cleansers | **Absolute opposition** — euthanizing is the maximum harm; the Preservers oppose every Cleanser vote | Preserver ships have on multiple recorded occasions **physically positioned themselves between Cleanser cruisers and target planets** — not for combat, for moral witness. The Cleansers respect the witness and proceed anyway; the Preservers grieve. |
| Defenders | **Ignored** — Preservers will not fight; Defenders will not listen | The Defenders consider the Preservers naive; the Preservers consider the Defenders fundamentally misaligned with the Migration's logic. They do not argue. They wave at each other in the corridor of the Council. |
| Deniers | **Active counter-evangelism** — Preservers go to Denier communities and *live among them*, demonstrating that Migration is choosing-life | This works occasionally. Some Denier converts go full Preserver after a few seasons of canopy-singing. |
| Hiders | **Cooperative align** — Hiders shield-and-stay (a leave-no-one-behind doctrine of a different shape); Preservers love the symmetry | Preservers will sometimes position their ships around cloaked species as living witnesses, augmenting the cloak's protection with visible presence. The Hiders find this charming. |

### 6.1 The Preserver vote — opposite of the Cleanser vote

Where the Cleansers vote to euthanize every grounded species (per [cleansers-as-ice-branch.md §6](cleansers-as-ice-branch.md)), the Preservers vote to **preserve every species' choice**:

- **Species that can migrate**: vote to *invite them to migrate, persistently, with no pressure beyond invitation.* Continue the work even when the species refuses; the invitation itself is a gift the Migration leaves behind. Preservers oppose any forced extraction.
- **Species that cannot migrate (grounded)**: vote to *cloak them, hide them, or witness them through their last days with dignity.* Preservers oppose every Cleanser vote. They will personally attend a species' last days where the cluster's Steward permits.
- **Proto-species**: vote to *leave them undisturbed.* The Preservers are the canonical strongest voice on *most species are better off left alone*. The proto-species' Persuader-victory baseline canon is *largely Preserver-driven* — the soft-power lobby for non-interference.
- **Furlings refusing to migrate (Defenders)**: vote to *let them stay, with sorrow.* Preservers will not euthanize fellow Furlings under any circumstance. They will visit Defender holdouts before the Culling, share a meal, sing together, and depart.

### 6.2 The canonical Preserver-vs-Cleanser scene

When a Council deliberation surfaces a grounded species (Slylandro, Chenjesu, Mycon, Mmrnmhrm, Taalo, etc.), the canonical Council-floor scene includes:

- **Cleanser argument**: "*Just to be safe.*" The species must be euthanized; the cycle risk is too high; the friendship is in the record; the vote remains.
- **Preserver counter-argument**: "*We will not be safer for having killed them. The Quiet you propose is the Quiet of a closed grave. Our Quiet must be the Quiet of a continuing song, even if some voices in it are not ours.*"

This exchange is canonical across multiple Council records. The Cleanser delivery: cold and measured. The Preserver delivery: warm and unhurried. Both sincere. The Council majority decides each case; the Steward's recommendation contributes. **The slice's species-by-species saves are each one a Preserver vote-victory over a Cleanser vote-loss.**

## 7. The Preservers in the slice

### 7.1 Where they appear

The Preservers are not crewmates on the Steward's Scout (the bridge has 2 Furlings — both warm-world per `crew-roster-redesign.md`). They appear in the slice as:

- **Ambassadors**: Preserver-faction Furlings stationed alongside or near Homesteader-by-necessity species. A canonical Preserver ambassador appears in the Slylandro storyline (see §7.2).
- **Council voices**: in the Steward's filed-recommendation Council scenes, a Preserver-faction Council voice surfaces alongside the Persuader, Compeller, and Cleanser voices. The Preserver voice argues the *preserve-the-choice* doctrine; the player's recommendation interacts with all four.
- **Hyperspace encounter**: at least one Canopy-class Preserver vessel is encountered in the cluster's hyperspace, where the player can dialog with the ship's captain. Combat is *possible but discouraged* (heavy reputation cost; see §5.3). The encounter showcases the gravity-and-jumping kit canonically.
- **Sanctuary planet**: a small Preserver canopy-village on a tree-rich world somewhere in the cluster. The Steward can visit, eat fruit with the Preservers, hear their choral music, leave with a small living-plant cutting as a personal token. Optional content; reputation-positive if visited.

### 7.2 Canonical NPC — **Lirin Pel-Sa**

**Name**: Lirin Pel-Sa. *Lirin* = green-related root in Furling botanical naming; *Pel-Sa* = present-generation Preserver naming-suffix (parallel to *Olwen-Sa* warm-world and *Velven* ice-branch canon).

**Role**: Preserver ambassador stationed near Beta Corvi (the Slylandro home system). Lirin is canonically the first Furling to visit the Slylandro for the *purpose of loving them into a choice* — they have been there for forty years of their own time, singing with the Slylandro in the upper troposphere of Lai-leh, sharing weather-songs, gently arguing for the Migration option to the few Slylandro who could conceivably be evacuated and for the Cloaking Satellite path to the rest.

**Voice**:
- Slow, warm, choral — Lirin speaks with multi-tonal overtones (Preservers have practiced choral-voice as a single individual; they layer their own voice with itself across resonance frequencies). The effect is unusual; the listener perceives Lirin as *more than one voice in agreement with themselves*.
- Plant-metaphors in speech. *"We will branch toward what is right."* *"The root of this question lies deeper than the conversation we are having today."* *"You are seeded with potential, Steward — let it grow."*
- Will not speak quickly. Will not raise their voice. Will not interrupt.
- Canonical first-meeting line to the Steward: *"Steward. Hello. I have been listening to the Slylandro sing about you for some weeks. They speak warmly. Come, share fruit with me. The Migration question is not solved by haste."*

**Slice canonical contributions**:
- Visits the Slylandro before the Cleanser arrives in Act 3. Lirin's prior diplomatic groundwork is part of *why the Cloaking Satellite path is viable* — the Slylandro have already chosen the cloak, partly because Lirin has spent decades preparing them for the choice.
- Available as a soft-power consult in the Steward's Council-recommendation scene for the Slylandro and Mycon decisions. Lirin's advice: *"Preserve their choice. They are not asking us to do violence for them; they are asking us to make space for them to decide."*
- Survives the slice and Migrates with the Preserver contingent. Last canonical appearance: Lirin sings a farewell to the Slylandro from a Canopy-class vessel orbiting Beta Corvi as the Migration window closes. The Slylandro song-in-response is twelve days long. Recorded for the Bio-Archive.

### 7.3 The canonical Preserver-vessel hyperspace encounter

**Encounter trigger**: cluster hyperspace traversal, at least one of two trigger conditions met:
- The Steward has filed at least one Council recommendation; or
- The Steward has visited Beta Corvi (any contact with Slylandro)

**Encounter shape**:
- A Canopy-class Preserver vessel — the *Long-Reach Of The Slow Branch* — appears in hyperspace ahead of the player and broadcasts a peaceful hail.
- Captain: **Veth Pel-Sa** (Lirin's elder sibling; also a Preserver; ship-captain rather than ambassador).
- Dialog: Veth invites the Steward to dock and share fruit. The Steward can:
  - **Dock** — visit the Canopy-class interior; canonical *first-look-at-Preserver-life* scene; warm interior light, choral music in the background, fruit-and-water meal; Veth offers the Steward a small living-plant cutting as a token. Reputation boost: Preserver standing ++.
  - **Decline politely** — Veth understands; ship departs with a song; small Persuader/Preserver standing ++ (the Preservers respect a polite no).
  - **Decline rudely** — Veth is still gentle; ship departs without song; Preserver standing slightly down; the dialog moment is canonically *one of the slice's small moral failures* if the player takes this path.
  - **Attack** — the Preserver ship enters its non-lethal combat kit. The encounter showcases the gravity-and-jumping kit. If the Steward presses the attack to a kill, **Preserver standing collapses** + **Persuader standing collapses** + **Compeller standing slightly up** (the Compellers found the action regrettable but doctrinally defensible — Compellers will say *"we would have preferred the Preservers were lighter on doctrine, but we acknowledge they did not give you any reason"*). The Steward's reputation across the slice's faction tracking takes a heavy hit. The Bio-Archive surfaces a Steward-Quiet-Ledger entry for Veth Pel-Sa.

The encounter is the slice's canonical *combat doctrine demo* for the Preservers. The player who fights gets to see the kit work; the player who docks gets to see the culture work. Both paths are content-positive.

### 7.4 The Preserver canopy-village (optional sanctuary)

A small Preserver settlement on a forest-canopy world (cluster location TBD; Image/Design's call which star to assign — a habitable green world with high forest canopy). The Steward can land and visit.

**What the player sees**:
- A village of woven-bark dwellings nestled in the upper branches of a vast tree-network
- Approximately 200 Preservers in residence; canopy-life visible — sunbathing platforms, choral practice groves, fruit-orchards on lower branches, small contemplation chambers carved into the trunks
- Mid-morning ambient: layered choral music drifting between dwellings
- **Mid-evening ambient**: silence; the Preservers eat slowly at sunset and conserve volume
- A small Furling-engineered tank visible at the village edge — *yes, that is a Taalo extraction tank*, the Preservers helped build the prototype. Vresh's tank on the Steward's ship is derived from work the Preservers contributed. The village has been hosting a Taalo guest for six years, exchanging slow-cognition-cultures with each other.

**Optional interactions**:
- Share fruit with a Preserver elder (Reputation ++)
- Listen to a choral practice (no choice; the player just listens for a few minutes; small standing boost across multiple factions including Persuaders, Hiders, and the Slylandro indirectly)
- Receive a living-plant cutting as a token (canonical inventory item; can be carried indefinitely; visually placed in the Steward's Common Room — the cutting goes well next to Vresh's tank)
- Speak with the resident Taalo guest if Vresh's *The Last Voice* quest has been completed favorably — the guest is a sibling-substrate-fragment of Vresh's colony; they recognize each other through proximity-empathic-resonance even at this range. Canonical reveal.
