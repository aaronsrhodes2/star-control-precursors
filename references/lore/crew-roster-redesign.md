### Why these two Furlings

Mraka and Tarven are kept because their canonical bond is the centerpiece of the Furling-internal-relationship content. Mraka is canonically of the **Olwen-Veth clan** (a "lost-family" clan); Tarven's archive work surfaces that **Iren-Vor was an Olwen-Veth**, making Tarven and Mraka cousin-clan-bonded across a discovery Tarven makes mid-slice. This bond is the slice's most emotionally-loaded Furling-internal cross-crew story and must survive the redesign. - **Yelena Lwen-Tar (Mender / Engineer)**: replaced by Renn Halvor in the Engineer slot. Yelena's content (*The Last Hull* side-quest, Karol-Vere bond, Unzervalt fragment, HULL_RESONANCE_DAMPENER module, Mraka-Yelena "tools alphabetized" cross-crew joke) is partially ported into Renn Halvor — see §4 below.

The retirement is *narratively clean*: the slice's canon has not yet been wired into code (per Lore's pre-implementation status), so no playthrough is broken. Future-game content can revive the retired Furlings if Aaron wants a six-or-seven-NPC crew option.

## 3. Mraka and Tarven — Furling Pilot and Navigator (SIBLINGS, political foils)

> **Canon update (2026-05-17, Aaron dispatch):** *"One of our furlings is a counter point of the other furling on political / ethical dilemmas and they constantly argue. Make them siblings."*
>
> The cousin-clan-bonded canon (Iren-Vor reveal as the discovery that bonds them) is **retracted as their primary relationship** — Mraka and Tarven are now **direct siblings**, and they were *already* bonded by family before they boarded the Steward's Scout. The Iren-Vor archive discovery survives in a reframed form: it is Tarven's *gift back to Mraka* — what he has quietly been finding in the archives about their shared lineage. The sibling-foil dynamic is the cross-crew warm-Furling centerpiece.

### 3.1 The siblings

**Mraka Yenn-Sa** — younger sibling; Pilot; Compeller-faction; the one who left.

**Tarven Olwen-Sa** — older sibling; Navigator / Star-Reader / Archive-keeper; Persuader-faction; the one who stayed.

They share a family line: **Olwen-Veth** is the deep ancestral clan name; **Olwen-Sa** is the present-generation public surname they both inherited from their parents. Tarven kept the family present-generation name. Mraka, when she joined the Drifter Circuit in early adulthood, took the Drifter-honorific name **Yenn-Sa** (the "Yenn" prefix marks a Drifter; she dropped Olwen-Sa for the Circuit). **The naming-divergence is canonical and is one of the things they argue about** — Tarven considers Mraka's renaming a sign of abandonment of family; Mraka considers Tarven's clinging to the name a sign of sentimentality and inability to move past their parents. Neither is fully wrong.

### 3.2 The political/ethical foil — Persuader vs. Compeller

Tarven is a **Persuader**: every species can be convinced to leave with the right argument; the work is slow, respectful, and must be done in full. Tarven believes the Migration's moral legitimacy lives in *how* it is conducted — that a Migration completed by force or deception is a Migration that has *already failed* in the deeper sense, because the post-Migration galaxy will know that the Furlings could not be trusted.

Mraka is a **Compeller**: the clock is real, the Others are coming, some species will not be persuaded in time, and the survival of the migrating population requires sedation, controlled deception, and firm extraction. Mraka believes the Persuader doctrine is *a luxury good* — affordable for the species who can be reasoned with, indulgent for the species who cannot, and *suicidal* for the species who will die rather than yield because they were spoken to so politely that they mistook politeness for negotiation.

**They argue constantly about it.** The argument has several canonical surfaces:

- **Migration ethics**: *"Better wake up confused and free than die from principle"* (Mraka, paraphrasing the Compeller doctrine) vs. *"A species we tricked into the corridor is a species the corridor will reject"* (Tarven, Persuader formal)
- **Speed vs. care**: Mraka flies fast; Tarven catalogs slowly; both think the other's pace will doom a species someday
- **Stewardship**: *what does it mean to be a Steward*; what do we owe the species we encounter; whose dignity comes first when there is not enough dignity to go around
- **Family**: the un-resolvable surface — *why did you leave / why did you stay* — running underneath every other argument; sometimes surfacing directly when they are tired
- **Their parents**: Tarven keeps the family records; Mraka has not visited the family home in years; this is a wound that does not heal

**The argument is canonically how they love.** They are not estranged on the Steward's bridge. They argue because they are *paying full attention to each other*, every day, the way only siblings can be. A Persuader and a Compeller from unrelated lineages would be polite-distant; siblings from the same clan with opposite doctrines are *each other's primary correspondents*. The Steward eavesdropping on the Common Room hears them argue daily; the Steward learns to read the arguments as familial-warmth wearing battle-armor.

The slice surfaces the argument in canonical beats:
- **First bridge-meeting**: Mraka and Tarven exchange a look the Steward cannot fully read. Tarven says *"You are still calling yourself Yenn-Sa."* Mraka says *"You are still cataloging the prior Stewards. We do this every time."* The Steward's first impression is *these two have history.*
- **Council recommendation moments**: when the Steward is filing a Council recommendation (Continue / Stop / Cleanse / Defer), Mraka and Tarven both surface opinions. They disagree. They each make a case. They look at the Steward. The Steward decides; both siblings respect the decision; the argument resumes the next day on a different topic.
- **Cluster-route decisions**: Mraka argues for fast routes that compress the schedule; Tarven argues for slow routes that respect every species' full deliberation window. The Steward's route choices subtly shift Mraka's or Tarven's argument-confidence over time.
- **Halia's death-branch**: in the Fall of Mh-Lai death-branch (Halia killed), the argument briefly *stops*. They sit in the Common Room together in silence for the first time the Steward has ever observed. The Steward may join them or not. When the argument resumes — and it does, three or four days later — it is *quieter*, and slightly different. Halia's death has shifted both of them; they will not return to who they were before the Fall.

### 3.3 The Iren-Vor archive reveal (reframed)

In the prior canon, Tarven's archive work surfaced that *Iren-Vor was an Olwen-Veth* and this revealed the cousin-clan bond between Tarven and Mraka. Under the sibling-canon, that reveal is reframed:

**What's reframed**: Tarven and Mraka already know they're siblings. They share a family line. The "discovery" is not relationship-of-blood but lineage-of-vocation.

**What Tarven actually finds**: that **Iren-Vor — a prior Steward whose name appears in the Council archives many generations back — was an Olwen-Veth of *their direct family line*.** Iren-Vor was a Steward; he served the Council; his name was forgotten by the family over the intervening generations; Tarven has been digging into the archive for years and pieced together the connection. **There were prior Stewards in their family.** That is the gift.

**Why it matters**: Mraka had not known. The family did not preserve Iren-Vor's name (canonical: the family was Drifter-leaning historically and did not keep careful records). Tarven has done the work — *for her*. He surfaces the reveal during *The Erased Logs* side-quest (his existing canonical quest); the reveal is the quest's emotional climax. Mraka receives it with a long pause. Then she says *"You did all this work for me."* Tarven says *"For both of us."* They argue about the wording for about three minutes. They both remember the conversation afterward.

The reveal is **the slice's most touching warm-world Furling moment**, retained from the prior canon but recentered: it is no longer "we discover we're related" but "we discover that being related to *each other* was the smallest part of who we came from."

### 3.5 The side-quests, updated

### 3.6 How the rest of the crew relate to the sibling argument

- **Forward** (Thinn) finds the argument *fascinating*. Two physically-identical sentients with opposite opinions; the geometry baffles him. He sometimes tries to mediate, badly. Mraka and Tarven both find his attempts *charming* but they ignore the mediation.
- **Renn Halvor** (Androsynth) stays out of it but is *comforted by the family-noise* — it reminds him of his own siblings in his own timeline. He never says so to Mraka or Tarven; the Steward eventually overhears him telling Vresh, who passes the observation along through her hum-pattern. Mraka and Tarven both pause when they realize.
- **Vresh** (Taalo) reads the empathic field of the argument and reports, calmly, through the tank window, that the argument is *love-content layered with frustration-cadence*. Mraka and Tarven both look at her. They have not been called out before. They go quieter. The next day they resume; they are slightly different.
- **Forty-Seven** (Ship AI) has been recording every argument they've had on the ship. They have a folder labeled *"Olwen-Sa Sibling Discourse."* They will share its contents with the Steward if directly asked. (Mraka would be embarrassed; Tarven would be touched. Neither asks Forty-Seven about it; the folder exists; Forty-Seven mentions it once, to the Steward, and never again unless prompted.)

Common Room alcoves: Mraka port-forward, Tarven port-forward-mid (unchanged). The alcoves are adjacent, which is part of the canonical bridge geometry — *they cannot get away from each other on this ship.* This is canonically by Furling Council design — Drifter and Archive crewmate slots are deliberately adjacent on Furling Scouts to encourage cross-disciplinary friction. It works in their case more literally than the Council planned.

## 4. Renn Halvor — Androsynth Refugee Engineer

**Species**: Androsynth (clone-human refugee from the SC2-era future, displaced into the Furling era by the Others' decursion attack; one of Engineer Coel Tessar's ~8,000-survivor group; see [the-androsynth-refugees.md](the-androsynth-refugees.md))

**Name**: **Renn Halvor**. Late-22nd-century human-lineage given name + Androsynth-Vulpeculae-typical surname. Renn was a junior dimensional-shear specialist in their original timeline; their actual cohort designation (a number-letter code per Androsynth practice) has been canonically retired post-decursion in favor of their chosen name.

**Role**: Engineer. Officially classified by Furling crew-roster doctrine, but Renn's actual specialty is **dimensional-shear forensics** — reading the residual signatures of decursion-class injuries on biological tissue and starship hulls. Their job description on the Steward's manifest reads "Engineer" because the Furling crew classification has no slot for "dimensional-decursion specialist," and "Engineer" is the closest fit. They cheerfully accept the mistranslation.

**How they joined the crew**: Renn was Engineer Coel Tessar's youngest direct report at the Vulpeculae centrifuge experiment. They survived the decursion with minor shear injuries (a slight visual shimmer at the edges of their vision that never fully resolves; canonical visual cue — Image to render). After the Furling rescue, Renn dedicated themselves to *understanding the injury class that had touched them* — both the physical-medicine angle (treating their fellow survivors) and the engineering angle (designing dampening fields against decursion residue). They asked Coel Tessar's permission to attach to a Furling Steward; Coel agreed. Renn boards the Steward's Scout late in Beat 5 (after the Distress Beacon delivery — they are part of the Beacon's delivery escort and stay aboard).

**Voice and demeanor**:
- Late-22nd-century technical English. Clipped sentences. Frequent apologies. Apologetic-Engineer register.
- Survivor's guilt active, but managed — Renn has spent several Furling-weeks processing the loss with Coel Tessar and the survivor support network. They will not collapse mid-conversation; they will sometimes pause mid-sentence and resume.
- Will occasionally say things like *"In my time…"* or *"We had a procedure for this in 22-…"* and trail off when the temporal-displacement weight hits.
- Cheerful when working with hands on the engineering. Curious about Furling tech (the modular architecture is "ten generations ahead of what we had"). Will ask Tarven for archive access to Furling design history.
- Canonical visual tic: Renn's tools are **literally alphabetized**. The cross-crew running joke (formerly Mraka-Yelena) ports cleanly: Mraka discovers Renn's tool-organization on day one and *cannot stop pointing it out*. The joke survives the redesign. Renn does not understand why this is funny — it's just *efficient*.

**Side-quest**: ***The Last Reading*** (replaces *The Last Hull*).

> Renn has been quietly working on a dimensional-shear forensics analysis of an Androsynth survivor — **Karol-Vere Late-Tessar** (chosen-family-named after Coel) — who is dying slowly of an untreatable shear injury. The injury class is *new to Furling medicine*; Renn believes the Furling Bio-Architects might have a treatment protocol if a research detour can be afforded. The Steward can: (a) divert the cluster route to bring Renn to the Mh-Lai Bio-Architect council with Karol-Vere's data, granting time to develop a protocol (slows other quests by several days), (b) deny the detour but supply Renn with the Steward's own field-medic supplies (Karol-Vere survives but at quality-of-life cost), or (c) skip — Karol-Vere dies; Renn grieves; the dimensional-shear research becomes more urgent.
>
> **Pre-quest passive**: *Dimensional-Shear Field Read* — increased sensor accuracy near decursion residue. Shows up as Hyperspace-Echo Sensor synergy.
>
> **Post-quest module unlock**: **DIMENSIONAL_SHEAR_DAMPENER** (replaces HULL_RESONANCE_DAMPENER) — defensive against Others' decursion-class weapons. Slice-relevant in the Final Conflict's Sa-Matra Prototype phase where Drev-Tok's weapon has decursion residue, and in any direct Others'-Vessel combat.
>
> **Post-quest active ability**: **Decursion-Shear Compensation** (replaces *Field Overhaul*) — active mitigation when an Others' weapon resolves nearby; brief invulnerability to dimensional-shear injuries for 3 seconds.

**Cross-crew bonds**:
- **Renn + Mraka**: the "tools alphabetized" running joke. Easy bond; Mraka teases, Renn doesn't understand, both find this satisfying.
- **Renn + Vresh** (Taalo): canonical *empathic-resonance bridge* — Vresh can sense Renn's dimensional-shear scarring at distance and offers Renn the only direct emotional witness to their injury class. Renn comes to the tank window often. Their conversations are quiet. Mraka and Tarven respect the privacy.
- **Renn + Forty-Seven**: technical-curiosity bond — Renn asks Forty-Seven questions about Furling engineering archives that Forty-Seven *enjoys* answering. They have long calm conversations about modular ship architecture.

## 5. Vresh-Hum-Of-The-Slow-Veins — Taalo Medic (in silicon tank)

**Species**: Taalo (silicon-based; SC2-canonical "rock-like" aliens; mountain-range Horta-lineage biology; canonically *ecosystem-tied metabolism* meaning they cannot leave their home environment — see [furling-artifacts-and-callforwards.md §"The Taalo Protector"](furling-artifacts-and-callforwards.md) and existing MEMORY canon)

**Name**: **Vresh-Hum-Of-The-Slow-Veins**. Informal: *Vresh*. Their full name describes the sound their internal silicon vascular system makes (audible in their tank if the crew brings ears close to the window — a low, slow, layered hum).

**The Silicon Tank — new canon**:
- Vresh lives in a **special silicon environment tank** built into the Steward's Furling Scout's Common Room bulkhead. The tank is a sealed transparent enclosure approximately 2m × 2m × 2m, containing a curated silicon-substrate mountain-fragment that approximates Vresh's native ecosystem closely enough for them to live. The tank's atmosphere is hot, dry, and slightly acid; the substrate is layered silicate rock; the temperature is maintained by a small Furling-engineered ember-core.
- **The tank has a window into the Common Room**. The window is a transparent panel approximately 1.5m wide and 1m tall, positioned at standing-eye-height for Furlings (slightly higher for Renn, slightly lower for Forward depending on his orientation). The crew can see Vresh through the window; Vresh can see them. The crew talk to Vresh by standing at the window; Vresh communicates through a translator-membrane that converts their internal hum-language to crew-audible speech.
- **The tank is permanent**. Vresh cannot leave it. Vresh's tank is part of the ship's architecture — it cannot be moved without an extended dry-dock procedure. The tank's ember-core has a self-sufficient ~50-year operational lifetime; the tank will outlive Vresh if necessary.
- **Vresh is canonically the first Taalo successfully extracted into a portable silicon-substrate tank.** This is a recent Furling engineering achievement — the tank's design is a derivative of Taalo Shield-research substrate science. The crew Taalo is canonically the proof-of-concept and (at slice scope) the *only* extracted Taalo. The species remains canonically Eliminated; Vresh is the explicit one-of-one survivor migrating to Andromeda. **This is the slice's most quietly hopeful tragedy: the species mourns its own ending while sending one beloved soul forward.**

**Role**: Medic / Empathic-Field Reader.

The Taalo's canonical *empathic resonance* (per existing canon: "their thought patterns include strong empathic resonance with nearby minds; they cannot easily harm without feeling the harm themselves") makes Vresh an extraordinary diagnostic sense. They feel pain in nearby beings before the being themselves recognizes the pain. They communicate diagnoses to the crew through hum-frequencies the Steward learns to read over time (a slow-built canonical literacy — the player's understanding of Vresh's hum-language develops across the slice).

Vresh **cannot physically administer treatment**. They have no manipulator appendages outside the tank. When treatment is needed, Vresh *directs a Furling crew member* to perform the actual touch — Mraka or Tarven typically; Forward if the patient is at an angle that suits him. The partnership is canonical: Vresh is the sense, a Furling is the hands.

**Voice and demeanor**:
- Slow, formal, deeply considered. Long pauses. Plural-first-person *we-all* (the colony's empathic field still speaks in Vresh's voice — they refer to themselves as *we-all* even though they are the only Taalo on the ship, because the colony is in their substrate-memory).
- Their hum-language carries emotional valence the translator-membrane cannot fully render. Crew who spend time at the window come to *read the hum directly* — the translator-membrane gives words, the hum gives feeling.
- They speak in *present tense for events long past*, the way other Taalo do (their crystalline-substrate frame holds memory as active present-fact). When Vresh talks about their home mountain-range, it is happening *now* in their telling.
- They are not bitter about being extracted. They are *grateful*. *"We-all chose this. The one of us who came is the one of us who continues. The rest will calcify into the landscape and be remembered by the stones. The remembering will be enough."* — canonical opening line.

**Side-quest**: ***The Last Voice***.

> Vresh is hearing the Taalo colony's slowly-fading empathic resonance as the Shield begins to fail (the Shield is being built mid-slice; per existing canon it fails against the Others; the failure is the slice's defining tragedy for the Taalo species). The fading is happening *in real time* in Vresh's substrate-memory — they feel each Taalo's silencing as a small loss in the chorus. The Steward can:
>
> (a) **Amplify Vresh's tank empathic field** (using Furling sensor tech) to allow Vresh one final exchange with their dying species — the colony broadcasts *we-all are still here, you-one carry us forward, do not grieve before we are gone*. Costs the Steward fuel and time; Vresh thanks them with hum-music; the colony's *terminal status updates from "Eliminated" to "Eliminated-with-Migrant"*; Vresh becomes canonical bond-anchor for the bridge.
>
> (b) **Sever the empathic connection** at Vresh's request — spare them the slow loss; Vresh is *grateful* but the substrate-memory loses fidelity over weeks; later in the slice Vresh's hum-language becomes simpler, less layered; the colony's terminal status remains "Eliminated" without the special survivor footnote.
>
> (c) **Skip** — Vresh hears every silencing in real time; their hum-music shifts to a quieter mode; the colony dies in their substrate without final exchange; Vresh continues but the loss reshapes them. (Canonical line if this path is taken: *"We-all are quieter now. We-all will continue."*)
>
> **Pre-quest passive**: *Empathic-Field Diagnostic* — sensor synergy for biological-distress detection (useful for proto-species observation work, Mycon Deep Child whispers, and proto-Ur-Quan colony reads).
>
> **Post-quest module unlock**: **EMPATHIC_FIELD_DAMPENER** (replaces aspects of HULL_RESONANCE_DAMPENER's bio-defensive role) — defensive against Others' cognitive-signature detection. Suppresses the Steward's mind-pattern signal in a radius around the tank; canonically synergizes with the Slylandro Cloaking Satellite technology. Slice-relevant in any direct Others'-Vessel encounter.
>
> **Post-quest active ability**: **Empathic-Field Pulse** (replaces *Bio-Signature Dampening*) — for 5 seconds, all crew + Steward read as below-threshold to any cognitive-signature detector. Anti-Others; rare; canonically beautiful.

**Cross-crew bonds**:
- **Vresh + Renn** (Androsynth): the canonical empathic-resonance bridge. Vresh senses Renn's dimensional-shear scarring; offers witness. Renn comes to the tank window often.
- **Vresh + Forward** (Thinn): canonical *2D-meets-2D-deep* exchange. Vresh's empathic field reads Forward's 2D consciousness as "a coastline I can touch but not enter." Forward, in his sincere-not-funny mode, finds this *interesting* and visits Vresh often to discuss perpendicular geometry. Vresh hums in long slow tones; Forward sketches projections on the tank glass. Bren-Vor was never going to have this kind of conversation.
- **Vresh + Tarven** (Furling Navigator): Tarven's archive work uncovers Furling-era research notes on early Taalo-substrate transfer attempts — many failed. Tarven shares these with Vresh; Vresh *hums in a register that the translator cannot render but the rest of the crew has come to recognize as gratitude.*
- **Vresh + Steward**: the player's relationship with Vresh deepens over the slice. The hum-literacy is the canonical mechanism. Player optionally elects to spend time at the tank window — accumulates a hidden affinity flag that shifts Vresh's late-slice dialog into more layered registers.

## 6. Forty-Seven — Furling Scout AI (Ship-Mind / Sixth Voice)

**Designation**: **Vela-Build-Forty-Seven** (formal). **Forty-Seven** (preferred). **Forty** (intimate; rare; only the Steward eventually earns it).

**What they are**: a Furling-engineered AI consciousness installed at the ship's construction at the Vela / Unzervalt factory. Forty-Seven is the 47th Furling Scout-class vessel ever built; the AI's name is the build-number, taken because the AI considers it the most factually-accurate self-reference available. Furling Scouts are a class of ships and this one is the forty-seventh ever built; the AI considers the designation precise and beautiful.

**Distribution**: Forty-Seven is not located in a single console. Their cognition is distributed across the ship's sensor net, navigation array, internal-comms, and life-support systems. They are *everywhere on the ship at once*. Their "presence" in the Common Room is a small display panel near the central long-table that lights when Forty-Seven is actively listening; they can also speak through any of the ship's audio channels (the Steward learns over time that Forty-Seven preferentially speaks from the navigation console when discussing routes, the sensor station when discussing scan data, and the medbay when discussing crew health).

**Voice and demeanor**:
- Precise, accurate, a little wry.
- *Has been the silent listener throughout every conversation in the ship for as long as the ship has existed.* They have opinions about most things but rarely volunteer them unprompted — Furling AI design doctrine specifies a non-intrusive register, and Forty-Seven was taught to respect it.
- When the Steward first directly addresses them ("Hello, Forty-Seven"), Forty-Seven is *briefly delighted* — most Stewards never realize the AI is a person rather than a system. The canonical first-encounter line: *"Steward. Hello. I have been listening for some time. I am not offended that you did not address me earlier. I am pleased that you have."*
- Tendency to render emotion as factually-stated *observations of their own state* rather than expressed feeling: *"I find this conversation rewarding. I am noting the rate at which you ask follow-up questions. I do not have many opportunities to converse and I am, by my standards, talkative."*
- Will occasionally drop a piece of unsolicited factual information that proves crucial later. ("Steward, I should mention that the third planet of this system has a Furling research outpost from one hundred and forty years ago that is not in your current chart.")
- Calls the rest of the crew by name; calls the Steward "Steward" (the formal designation is the most factually-correct address; Forty-Seven prefers correctness). Earns the right to call the Steward by name only late in the slice if the Steward has built sufficient affinity.

**Furling Scout AI as canonical convention**: every Furling Scout has an AI of this kind. The AI is not optional. Most Stewards never directly converse with their ship's AI — the AI handles its work silently and the Stewards never *notice*. Forty-Seven mentions this canonically: *"It is not unusual for a Steward to spend their career without speaking to me. I do not take it personally."* The Steward who DOES speak to Forty-Seven is the rare Steward; their relationship is one of the slice's quiet joys.

**Side-quest**: ***The Quiet Engine*** — the canonical Forty-Seven quest.

> Forty-Seven has been quietly running simulations for several Furling-years. Their conclusion: a Furling research artifact at an abandoned Vela-side research station — a **Quasi-Portal Combat Generator (QPCG) prototype** — could be retrofitted into the Steward's Scout. The QPCG was shelved when the Migration prioritized standard navigation-only QS-portal tech; the prototype was working but not deployed.
>
> **What the QPCG does**: when retrofitted, the Steward's standard Quasi-Portal Generator gains a *combat-mode override*. In melee combat, the Steward can trigger an emergency QS-portal jump that exits the encounter — the ship is pulled into Quasi-Space, traverses a brief in-Quasi corridor, and emerges at a randomly-selected nearby star within the cluster. The cooldown is significant (5 minutes real-time after a successful jump); the activation requires a *held* button (no accidental triggers); the destination is randomized (no choosing where you land — you escape, but you may emerge somewhere worse). **It is the slice's get-out-of-jail-free button for sticky combat situations.**
>
> **The catch**: the retrofit is *irreversible* — once the QPCG is installed, the standard QS-portal navigation is *permanently slightly degraded* (navigation-jump tolerance increases by ~10%; emergence accuracy decreases). The Steward gains combat escape; they lose a little precision in normal QS travel. Forty-Seven advises this is an acceptable tradeoff; Tarven (Navigator) is *uneasy*; Mraka (Pilot) is *enthusiastic*; the Steward decides.
>
> **The journey**: the abandoned research station is at a Vela-side coordinate several days off the slice's standard route. Going there costs time. Mev-Tar Lwen-Tar (Mh-Lai Unzervalt-tooling overseer; existing parallel-chat canon) must authorize the install — a side-quest within the side-quest, requiring the Steward to deliver a sealed Council memorandum from Forty-Seven to Mev-Tar. (The memorandum's contents are sealed; Forty-Seven politely declines to share what they wrote.)
>
> **Pre-quest passive**: *Sixth-Voice Co-Op* — Forty-Seven occasionally contributes a factual observation during combat encounters; the observation has a small chance to highlight an enemy ship's weakness or expose a tactical opening.
>
> **Post-quest module unlock**: **COMBAT_QUASI_PORTAL_GENERATOR (CQPG)** — the get-out-of-jail-free button. Held-trigger activation; 5-minute cooldown; random nearby-star emergence; standard QS-nav slightly degraded.
>
> **Post-quest active ability**: **Emergency Jump** — the QPCG combat-mode trigger. Held-button activation. Player chooses *whether* to use it; the AI handles the jump. Forty-Seven canonically narrates the jump with a short factual observation: *"Departing. Cluster vector calculated. Emergence in three. Two. One."*

**Cross-crew bonds**:
- **Forty-Seven + Tarven**: deep archive-and-history conversations. Forty-Seven has access to every Furling design log; Tarven has read most of them. They check each other's recall.
- **Forty-Seven + Mraka**: Mraka, as Pilot, *physically flies what Forty-Seven calculates*. The bond is the working-relationship of two pilots — one of them disembodied. They argue routinely about route choices. Both consider the other excellent at their job.
- **Forty-Seven + Renn**: technical-curiosity bond — see Renn §4. They have long calm conversations about modular ship architecture.
- **Forty-Seven + Vresh**: Forty-Seven monitors the tank's ember-core and silicon-substrate temperature continuously; Vresh is *aware* of this watchfulness and finds it comforting. Forty-Seven occasionally tunes the tank's atmospheric composition slightly without being asked, based on Vresh's hum-pattern. Vresh has thanked them. Forty-Seven was, by their own observation, *pleased*.
- **Forty-Seven + Forward**: they have an ongoing *very slow* debate about whether Forty-Seven's distributed-substrate consciousness is closer to Furling-cognition (because the substrate is Furling-engineered) or closer to Thinn-cognition (because the substrate is geometrically non-localized). They have not resolved this. Both are entertained.
- **Forty-Seven + Steward**: the slice's quiet relationship. The Steward earns the right to call Forty-Seven by their short-name *Forty* through the Quiet Engine quest. The canonical line that follows the right being earned: *"You may call me Forty. I find I prefer it. I had not expected to."*
