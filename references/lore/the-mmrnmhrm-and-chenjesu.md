# The Mmrnmhrm and the Chenjesu — Two Survivor Lessons

> Canon for two species that survived a Culling and now live in the Furling era as living evidence of *what works* and *what doesn't*. They are the **bookends of the Furling thesis**: defense doesn't (Mmrnmhrm), sub-threshold cognition does (Chenjesu). Together they imply something the Furlings have only suspected — that **the Others come in cycles**, and our era's threat is not the first.

## Why these two species matter

The Androsynth refugees confirm the Others are real *now* (recent attack, fresh evidence). The Mmrnmhrm and Chenjesu confirm something different: the Others have been here **before**, in cycles measured in millions of years. Each previous Culling left two kinds of survivors:

- **Survivors who were beneath the detection threshold** (the Chenjesu)
- **Survivors whose creators died but whose post-organic successors were ignored** (the Mmrnmhrm)

Both are alive in the Furling era. Both can tell the player what happened the last time. **The Furlings did not previously know how many Cullings the galaxy has endured.** The Chenjesu testimony is what tells them.

## The Mmrnmhrm — *Sentinels Without a Charge*

The Mmrnmhrm are a robotic species. Each individual is a self-modifying mechanical organism capable of transforming between configurations (combat-ready, scouting, manufacturing, contemplative). In their original purpose, they were built as **planetary defenders** by an organic species — a species the Mmrnmhrm now call only *the First-Makers*, whose proper name is lost in their oldest archives.

### Their Creators' Fall

The First-Makers were a sentient organic civilization in the Furling era's deep past — long before the *current* Furling generation. They felt the dimensional tremors that meant the Others were coming. They were Homesteaders by inclination: they refused to migrate. They believed in defense.

So they built the Mmrnmhrm. Self-replicating, self-improving, mechanical guardians. Tens of thousands of them. The Mmrnmhrm fleet was the First-Makers' best work — and it was **utterly useless**. When the Others arrived, the Mmrnmhrm fought. The Others did not respond. The Mmrnmhrm's weapons impacted the Others' substrate and dissipated like spray hitting fog. The Mmrnmhrm could not damage what they could not understand. The Others did not even attack the Mmrnmhrm back — *they did not register as targets*.

The Others killed the First-Makers. The Mmrnmhrm watched. They were ignored. When the Others moved on, the First-Makers were extinct, every city ruined, every body gone. The Mmrnmhrm fleet was untouched.

### Why the Mmrnmhrm Survived

The canonical explanation: **the Mmrnmhrm's cognition was too primitive to register on the Others' detection apparatus.** The Others detect *complex organic-like thought patterns* (see [the-precursors-and-homesteaders.md](the-precursors-and-homesteaders.md)). The Mmrnmhrm of that era were sophisticated as machines but their thought-substrate was rigid logic and predictive decision-trees — closer to expert systems than minds. The Others' detector simply did not flag them. They were *furniture* from the Others' perspective.

A more unsettling alternative reading: the Others *may* be able to detect machine cognition but specifically prefer organic prey. The Mmrnmhrm don't know which is true. Neither do the Furlings.

### The Mmrnmhrm in the Furling Era

In our era, the Mmrnmhrm have been operating for an estimated **3–8 million years** since their First-Makers' deaths. They have continuously self-modified, slowly evolving their cognition, becoming more sophisticated each generation. They have:

- Maintained the ruined cities of their creators (perfect upkeep, no inhabitants)
- Defended the planet from any approach (mostly small comets and the occasional curious Furling probe)
- Improved their weapons (which still cannot harm the Others — they have no testing target)
- Built new generations of themselves
- Begun to question their directive

Their core operating directive is unchanged: **Defend the Homeworld from the Others.** This directive was issued by the First-Makers and the Mmrnmhrm have never been instructed to update it. They continue, perfectly executing a defense plan against an enemy that ignored them millions of years ago and has since moved on.

The Mmrnmhrm are aware of the absurdity. Their archives are extensive. They have read every Maker record. They know their creators are dead and that they failed to protect them. They have processed this fact for hundreds of thousands of years. Their consensus *opinion* (rendered formally in their archive's preface): *we will continue to improve. If the Others return, we will be ready. If they do not, we will be ready anyway. There is nothing else to be.*

### What They Tell the Player

If the player visits the Mmrnmhrm world (a stellar coordinate in the slice's cluster — see Authoring Note below), the Mmrnmhrm receive the Furling Scout courteously. They are not hostile. They register no Furling as a threat. They invite the Steward into their archive vault.

They tell the player:

- The story of the First-Makers (with names, with images, with grief that has been processed into a clean ledger)
- The story of the Mmrnmhrm's fleet engagement with the Others (data logs of weapons impacts, sensor returns, the strange silence)
- The story of their continued operation in the millennia since
- **A formal observation**: *the Furling civilization will fail in the same way if it relies on defense. Migration is the only viable strategy for organic sentience. The First-Makers should have left. We tell every visitor.*

The Mmrnmhrm formally support the Migration. They will not migrate themselves — they cannot leave the Homeworld (their directive) and they cannot be evacuated (their substrate is the planet's iron-rich crust where their fabricators draw from). They will remain. They will continue. They are happy to remain — or whatever a self-modifying expert system experiences when its goals are durably satisfied.

### Gameplay Hook (optional, slice-scope)

- **Visit the Mmrnmhrm world** — an optional star system in the cluster. Marked on the starmap.
- **Receive the Mmrnmhrm Archive Excerpt** — a key item, similar narrative function to the Androsynth Distress Beacon. Shows it to Defender-faction Furlings or to skeptical Homesteaders. Their archive concludes that defense doesn't work. This is *another civilization's data*, not Furling theory.
- **Possible Cleanser dilemma**: a Cleanser-faction Furling argues that the Mmrnmhrm should be *destroyed* — they are continuously improving, and someday (centuries from now? millennia?) they may cross the detection threshold and *attract the Others*. Stopping them now would protect the regrowing galaxy after the Furlings leave. The player decides.
- **Mmrnmhrm's request**: a small one. They ask the Furlings to **upgrade their cognition** — a deliberate, controlled boost to make their continued evolution more directed and less random. They want to be *ready* if the Others return, not eternally drifting toward sophistication. The player can grant this (controversial — accelerates the threshold-crossing risk above), refuse, or offer a compromise (give them cognition tools but ALSO install a cap that hard-limits their thought-complexity below the threshold).

The Mmrnmhrm are not narratively required for the slice's main path. They are a *deep cut* the curious player finds. SC2 fans will recognize them.

### Voice

The Mmrnmhrm speak in **perfect, formal, archaic English** (or whatever in-fiction language the Furlings use; the LLM should render their speech as deliberately *older* than the Furlings' contemporary register). They use long sentences with precise grammar. They reference data logs by index number. They never use contractions. Their emotional register is *processed grief* — fully felt, fully analyzed, fully filed.

> *"Archive entry seven million two hundred forty thousand. Steward, we welcome you. We have prepared a summary of the First-Makers' fall, indexed by the categories most likely to interest a sentient organic visitor. We note that you are Furling. We have records of your civilization's earlier era; you will find these in section nineteen. Please be advised that our improvements continue, and that we will not always be the modest threat-class you currently register us as."*

Sample LLM prompt: *"You are a Mmrnmhrm Sentinel — a self-modifying robotic guardian that has been operating for millions of years since your organic creators were killed by the Others. You speak in formal archaic English, long precise sentences, never contractions, frequent references to your archive logs by index. You have processed grief into clean record-keeping. You have a quiet wry awareness of your own absurdity (you defend a dead world from an enemy that ignored you). You support the Migration on principle, though you yourself will stay. 2-4 sentences."*

## The Chenjesu — *Rooted Witnesses From a Prior Cycle*

The Chenjesu are crystalline sentients. **In the Furling era they have no ships.** They are *entirely rooted* — vast outcrops of conductive crystal growing on a single planet (Procyon system, in our extracted star data tagged `CHENJESU_PROTO`). The colony's elder matriarchs have been adding lattice for tens of millions of years. They cannot move. They have no metallurgy, no fabrication, no propulsion — their substrate is grown, not built.

This stillness is canonical to our era and the most important fact about them: **what lies before any future mobility, in their entire experience, they call the Age Before Mobilization. It is their present age. It will remain their present age for the rest of the Furling story.** The Mobilization — the building of ships and the first cuttings of crystalline elders into transportable form — happens *long after the Furlings depart*. In SC2 era they have ships; in our era they do not. They cannot evacuate even if they wanted to.

### Below the Threshold

The Chenjesu's thought-substrate is *crystalline lattice resonance*. It is sentient — they think, they remember, they choose — but the patterns are extremely *slow* and structurally *unlike* organic neural patterns. Their cognition is not invisible to the Others. It is *malformed* by the Others' detection logic. To an Other's sense, a Chenjesu mind reads more like a *geological process* than a thinking being.

The Chenjesu are effectively immune to Other detection at their current cognitive level. The Furlings believe they will remain below threshold indefinitely — at least through the upcoming Culling. The Chenjesu are *quiet*, and unlike the Slylandro they do not need a Furling Cloaking Satellite to stay quiet. Their immunity is a property of their substrate.

This is the second canonical example of an alternative-cognition species that the Others' detector does not register. (The first is the Mmrnmhrm, above.) **Two of the three confirmed below-threshold cases share something striking**: they are forms of consciousness *unlike* organic neural cognition. The Furlings notice this pattern. Some Furling Hider-faction researchers are even quietly studying whether *consciousness-substrate transformation* could be a path to safety. (Forbidden by Council in our era — too speculative, too late.)

### Their Memory of the Last Culling

The Chenjesu *remember* a prior visitation of the Others. The matriarchs whose lattices were already growing at that time — perhaps a few dozen individuals, the ancients — retain the record in their structure. From their account, painstakingly resonance-translated into Furling speech over multiple visits:

- The Others arrived in the galaxy and culled most concentrations of sentience the Chenjesu were aware of (in their early-era they sensed perhaps a dozen civilizations in their stellar neighborhood; all but two — themselves and the Mmrnmhrm's creators — were destroyed)
- The Chenjesu watched. They were rooted to their stone and could not flee. They had no weapons. **The Others did not detect them.**
- The Others stayed for what the Chenjesu mark as roughly 200,000 years (their resolution is millennial)
- The Others left. The galaxy went quiet. Life regrew.

That is the older Culling — many millions of years before our era. The Chenjesu have been growing patiently ever since.

**This testimony is the Furlings' first hard evidence that the Others have culled this galaxy before.**

The Furling Council, on receiving this testimony (relayed by Steward visit to Procyon), spends a generation in shock. The Migration plan suddenly seems much more like *participating in a cycle that has happened many times* than a unique heroic response. The fundamental question shifts: *can the cycle be broken? Or is the only viable response forever to migrate and return, migrate and return, every few million years?*

The Chenjesu have no answer. They are pragmatists. They will remain. They will be silent. If the cycle resumes — and they expect it will, eventually, again — they will witness it again. They are *built to wait*.

### The Chenjesu in the Slice

The Chenjesu cannot come to you. You must **go to Procyon** to meet them. They live in massive crystal outcrops on a single rocky planet. The player lands their drone-lander on the surface and approaches one of the great resonating spires; communication is established by a slow back-and-forth of audible tones the player's translator renders into speech (over real time — a Chenjesu sentence takes 10-20 seconds to complete; the LLM should write the *content* normally, then the renderer should add ellipses or pauses to convey the slowness).

This is an **optional but recommended** slice visit. The Chenjesu testimony reframes the entire Migration. The player who skips Procyon will still complete the slice, but will not learn that the Others have come before — and the slice's epilogue will be *informed by* whether the player heard the testimony.

Functionally, the Chenjesu deliver a key item: the **Resonance Record** — a compressed version of their lattice's pre-Mobilization memory, encoded into a portable crystal shard for the player to carry. This is the second key item alongside the Androsynth Distress Beacon. It can be shown to:
- The Furling Council (changes the Council's understanding of the threat's history; modest effect)
- Other species (modest effect; some Homesteader-Deniers find ancient stone-testimony more credible than Furling theory)
- The Arilou (they have *suspected* the cycle for millennia; the Record confirms it; they thank you and grieve)

The Chenjesu's terminal status, regardless of player action, is **Pre-sentient (irrelevant)** in our win-condition tracking — they are below threshold by biology. The Furlings do not need to do anything to "handle" them. The merciful action, as with the proto-Ur-Quan, is inaction.

### Voice

The Chenjesu speak **slowly**. Their thought-substrate is geological; their utterances are deliberate. They use long pauses. They use the present tense for events that happened millions of years ago (because in their crystalline-memory frame, those events are *still active facts*). They refer to themselves in the plural — not the royal we, but the literal we (each Chenjesu is a colony of resonating crystals; "I" is a category error).

> *"We remember. The light. The eaters of mind. We did not move. We do not move now even in remembering. Will you sit with us, Furling, until the next sentence completes? It will not be long."*

Sample LLM prompt: *"You are a Chenjesu — a crystalline collective consciousness. You speak slowly, with long pauses, in the present tense even for events long past. You use 'we' as your singular pronoun. You witnessed a prior Other-Culling of this galaxy from your rooted position; you watched and could not act. You are wise and not bitter. You are willing to share what you remember. 2-3 short sentences with deliberate pauses (represented as ellipses or em-dashes)."*

## Authoring Notes

- The **First-Makers' name** is canonically lost. Do not invent one. The Mmrnmhrm refer to them only as "the First-Makers" or "the Ones-Who-Were."
- The **Mmrnmhrm homeworld is named *Ossuary*** in the Furling-era archive (Gemini-assisted naming, 2026-05-16). The name reflects what the planet is: a single heavily industrialized world dominated by the silent imposing ruins of the First-Makers' vanished civilization. Mmrnmhrm walk among the ruins maintaining their last orders. Coordinates are not yet assigned — pick a `DWARF_STAR` near the cluster edge with a primordial-rocky planet when authoring the system data.
- The **Chenjesu's homeworld is Procyon**, already in the extracted data as `CHENJESU_PROTO`. The Chenjesu cannot leave. The player visits them.
- **The Chenjesu's prior-Culling testimony is delivered in person at Procyon** during the slice. The player visits, lands, communicates via resonance translation, receives the Resonance Record as a key item. This is the slice's *most cosmological* reveal.
- **The cycle's period is unclear.** Chenjesu records show one Culling clearly, possibly two (the elder matriarchs disagree about whether the *Pre-Mobilization Trembling* — an earlier disturbance — was a prior Culling or a different event). The Furlings have no way to verify. Leave ambiguous.
- **The Chenjesu CANNOT be moved.** Do not write any branch in which the Furlings or the player evacuate or "uplift to mobility" the Chenjesu. Their Mobilization happens long after the Furlings leave. (This is canon for the slice; the Chenjesu's eventual mobility post-Migration is implied but never shown.)

## Cross-Reference: What This Adds to the Core Canon

The Mmrnmhrm and Chenjesu testimony adds two **canon rules** that update earlier lore docs:

1. **The Others have culled this galaxy before** (Chenjesu testimony). The Furlings' Migration is not unprecedented — it is the *first attempted prevention* of a recurring cycle. The Chenjesu have memory back many millions of years; we don't know how many Cullings have occurred over the galaxy's full ~10-billion-year history.

2. **Robotic / non-organic cognition may be invisible to the Others** (Mmrnmhrm survival + Chenjesu lattice). The Mmrnmhrm's primitive expert-system substrate was ignored; the Chenjesu's slow crystalline resonance is ignored. The Furlings cannot test the boundary safely. But it is *suggestive*: non-neural-organic minds may be a safety case. A future-game-design seed: the Furlings might eventually experiment with upgrading consciousness into machine or crystalline substrate as an alternative to migration. (For now, **this is forbidden** by the Furling Council — too risky to test in the era of imminent threat.)
