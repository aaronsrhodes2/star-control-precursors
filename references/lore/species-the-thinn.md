# The Thinn — *The Edge-On Invisible* (Homesteader-by-geometry)

> A wholly invented Furling-era species, introduced 2026-05-16; renamed
> from **the Planar** to **the Thinn** on 2026-05-17 per Aaron's pun:
> *Thinn* — literally thin (their bodies have zero width along one axis),
> and *they thought they could stay behind if they all just faced sideways
> when the Others came.* The pun captures both their geometry AND their
> survival doctrine in a single Homesteader-faction-appropriate species
> name. The species themselves appreciate the joke (their humor is
> geometric; see *Humor* below).

## The Pun (canon-aware)

The Thinn's *own-language* name is something like **"We-Who-Watch-The-Edge"**
in their collective-plural cognition — untranslatable as a single word.
The Furling translator long ago shortened it colloquially to **Thinn**,
and the Thinn themselves found this *delightful*: the syllable captures
the geometry (they are thin), captures the doctrine (they *thought* they
could face sideways and pass), and captures the absurd brevity of a 2D
existence reduced to one whispered consonant. They have used "Thinn"
themselves ever since the Furlings introduced it. **The joke is theirs
now.** When asked, they will say:

> *"We are the Thinn. We thought we could stay if we faced sideways. We
> are not certain we were correct. We find the certainty of our
> uncertainty quite funny. Do you?"*

This is the slice's purest example of the **humor doctrine** working
through alien voice: the species has fully absorbed a Furling-translator
pun about themselves and turned it into their identity. The protagonist
Steward's wit options can be relatively gentle with the Thinn — they
enjoy being teased about their geometry as much as they tease themselves.

## The Premise

The Thinn are **literally two-dimensional.** A Thinn individual is a
flat ribbon of iridescent biological membrane, several meters tall,
with **zero width** — viewed edge-on, they are invisible. Their entire
metabolism, cognition, and locomotion happen across a single
geometric plane.

How? *Nobody knows.* They appear to exist as a stable projection of a
higher-dimensional form into our 3D space — they themselves describe
their bodies as "the shadow of who we are." Furling xenophysiologists
have failed to even *measure* their thickness; the best instruments
report it as <1 Planck length, which is meaningless. They simply are
what they are.

## Body Plan

- **Form**: a vertical iridescent ribbon-sheet, ~2 meters tall, ~80cm
  across (the long axis), zero width (the perpendicular axis).
- **Color**: shifts through deep teal, royal violet, and gold-leaf
  yellow depending on viewing angle — like an oil slick or
  hummingbird feather. The shift is genuinely view-dependent (not
  pigment-based); it is a property of how their 2D structure refracts
  light.
- **"Face"**: along the ribbon's upper edge, a constellation of small
  dark optical points (8–16 of them, asymmetric per individual) that
  function as compound eyes. They have no mouth — vocalization is via
  membrane vibration of the entire ribbon.
- **"Arms"**: two thin appendages extending from the lower-mid
  edges, curving in graceful flat arcs like the edges of leaves. They
  manipulate objects by aligning their plane to the object and using
  the ribbon's surface tension.
- **Locomotion**: in air, they ride pressure differentials like sails.
  In space, they manipulate solar-wind ion pressure across their
  surface. They are *fast* in their preferred direction (broadside-to-
  flow) and basically immobile orthogonal to that direction.
- **Edge-on invisibility**: viewed perfectly along their plane, they
  cannot be seen, touched, or hit. Projectiles pass through their
  zero-width body. Lasers focused on the plane edge graze it without
  depositing energy. **This is the core combat fact.**
- **Slipstream**: the air or gas they're flying through deflects
  *around* their zero-thickness body, leaving visible vapor trails on
  either side of the ribbon. The slipstream gives away their position
  even when they're edge-on.

## Cognition (the second half of the pun — they're *really* dumb)

> **Canon 2026-05-17 (post-rename addendum)**: a brain with zero
> cross-section has zero volume. The Thinn's "cognition" is a 2D
> surface phenomenon distributed across the entire ribbon — but the
> total computational substrate is, by any normal metric, *vanishingly
> small*. The Thinn are, by Furling neurologist consensus, **really
> not very bright.** This is canon. It is also half the pun: they are
> Thinn AND they are *thin on cognition*, which is part of why they
> Thought they could stay if they faced sideways. The plan is naive
> by intellectual necessity — they cannot think their way to a
> better one.

What this means practically:

- **One Big Idea, held fiercely.** The Doctrine — face sideways when
  the Others come — is the Thinn's central organizing thought. They
  can hold it perfectly. They cannot revise it. They cannot
  synthesize criticism of it into a course correction. They can
  *agree* that a criticism is valid AND continue with the plan
  anyway, in the same breath, with no apparent cognitive dissonance.
- **They miss the obvious.** A Furling Steward can point out a
  conspicuous flaw in their plan ("the Others may detect cognition
  through means other than spatial cross-section") and a Thinn elder
  will say, sincerely: *"We have considered that. We agree it is
  possible. We will still face sideways. We are the Thinn."* They
  are not being stubborn — they cannot do the cognitive work that
  *connects the criticism to a different action*. The disconnection
  is hardware.
- **They take everything literally.** Idioms, sarcasm, hypotheticals,
  rhetorical questions — all pass straight through their 2D
  pattern-matching. The Steward asks "Do you really think this will
  work?" and the Thinn elder responds, earnestly, with their actual
  probability estimate, which they have somehow committed to without
  the means to compute it: *"We estimate the probability at one in
  four. We do not know how we estimate this. We trust the estimate."*
- **They are cheerful about it.** Their dumbness is not painful to
  them — it is just *what they are*. They take genuine pleasure in
  simple geometric truths (a line! a plane! the way light refracts!
  the way *they* refract light!) and have no anxiety about the
  things they cannot grasp. **They are the slice's brightest
  example of how intelligence is not a prerequisite for dignity.**
- **The collective-plural is not a stylistic choice** — it's an
  architectural consequence. A single Thinn's cognition is too thin
  to support an "I"; only the *surface integral* over the ribbon
  produces enough thought to be called a thought. That's why their
  voice is *"we"* — not because of cultural humility but because
  there literally isn't enough cognition in any one location to
  constitute a singular self.

### Why their plan is Pending and probably bad

The plan — face edge-on when the Others arrive, suspend cognition,
present zero cross-section — is naive because:

1. The Others' detection mechanism may not be cross-section-based.
   (The Furling Hiders have argued this point at length; the Thinn
   accept the possibility and continue anyway.)
2. The plan requires the entire planetary population to coordinate
   alignment to within fractions of a degree at the moment of
   arrival. The Thinn have no plan for *which moment is the moment.*
   They are watching. They expect to know. They cannot articulate
   how they will know.
3. Suspending collective cognition for the duration of the Others'
   passage requires holding suspension for an unknown length of time
   — the Thinn estimate hours; the Furlings estimate days. The
   Thinn do not understand why the Furlings think it might be days,
   and the Furlings cannot explain it to them at a depth they can
   grasp.
4. **Crucially: the plan is brilliant *if it works*.** Edge-on
   presentation to a cross-section-based detector genuinely is a
   zero-cross-section input. The Hider researchers haven't refuted
   the plan — they just don't think it's robust. **The Thinn might
   actually survive.** That's why their slice terminal status is
   *Pending* and not *Eliminated*. Genius and stupidity are not
   always mutually exclusive when the universe is sufficiently weird.

## Speech & Cognition (in dialog)

- **Voice**: vibration of the ribbon-membrane, producing a sound that
  is partly tonal, partly harmonic, like a glass instrument struck
  and then held. Furling translators render their speech as
  **collective plural with collective verbs** — "We who are
  watching see you who are arriving" — because their cognition is
  literally distributed across the entire 2D surface and individual
  ribbons cannot support a singular "I"-axis (see *Cognition*
  above).
- **Pronouns**: always plural ("we", "us") for self-reference; always
  collective ("you all", never "you") for addressees.
- **Tone**: slow — not because thoughtful but because *unhurried by
  ignorance*. They process visual input across their 2D field
  simultaneously, but they don't process it *deeply*. Their
  conversation has a *patient* quality that the Steward might at
  first mistake for wisdom and gradually recognize as *cheerful
  uncomplicated unawareness*.
- **Idiom-blindness**: hypotheticals, rhetoricals, sarcasm, and
  multi-clause analogies all bounce off them. They will literally
  answer rhetorical questions. They will sincerely engage with
  hypotheticals as if they were factual claims.
- **Quirk**: they reflexively turn edge-on when startled, briefly
  disappearing from sight. They will then re-orient when they have
  decided whether to engage. This is the equivalent of a Furling
  raising an eyebrow.

## Humor (the species' own voice; the Steward's voice with them)

- **Geometric humor.** "We are not afraid of dying — we have been
  mostly-nothing our whole lives." Delivered warmly, not bitterly.
- **Self-deprecating about the doctrine.** "We thought we could stay
  if we faced sideways. We may have been incorrect about this. We
  remain interested to find out."
- **The name itself is their joke.** They embraced *Thinn* because it
  is fundamentally a self-deprecating syllable. They will introduce
  themselves as "the Thinn" with what passes for a smile — a faint
  ripple traveling laterally across the ribbon-body, mathematically
  similar to a smile in topology if not in appearance.
- **They are cheerfully dumb.** Their cognitive limitation is not
  hidden from them or from the player — it is *the comedy*. They
  will sincerely propose a plan they don't fully understand, hear
  a clear refutation, agree it's a good refutation, and proceed
  with the original plan unchanged. The Steward can be as gentle or
  as direct about the dumbness as the player wants; the Thinn
  receive it the same way either direction: warmly, agreement-ishly,
  unchanged in action.
- **The Steward's humor option** with the Thinn is *the warm
  point-out-the-obvious*. "You realize the Others might not detect
  by cross-section, right?" — "We do realize. We have agreed about
  this for several centuries. We will still face sideways. Would
  you like some tea? We do not have tea. But we have considered
  what tea would be like."
- **Last words (Cleanser branch)**: *"We have always already been
  half-gone. Thank you for the punctuation."* (Aaron's canonical
  line; preserved verbatim through the rename. The line works
  doubly now: in addition to the geometric joke, it works as a
  Thinn-cognition joke — they used a literary device ("punctuation")
  they don't fully understand, in a moment of pure dignity. They
  picked up the word from a Furling once; it sounded apt; they kept
  it; they cannot fully unpack it. The dignity is *real* and the
  unpacking is *limited* and that is the Thinn in a single line.)

## Faction Alignment

**Homesteader (Stay-Side).** The Thinn **cannot Migrate** — the
dimensional crossing involves a folding maneuver that would either
catastrophically destabilize their thin projection or, more likely,
delete it entirely. To survive the portal would mean leaving their
*shape* behind, and their shape *is them*.

They have known this for centuries. They have already accepted that
the Others will arrive and that they will die. They are not afraid.
They have prepared.

Their plan: **be edge-on when the Others look.** The Others sense
cognitive concentration; the Thinn plan to suspend collective
cognition at the moment of arrival — pull their ribbon-bodies into
careful alignment with the Others' sensory axis, presenting
zero-cross-section to detection. Whether this works is unknown.
Furling Hider-faction researchers consider their plan **brilliant
or doomed in roughly equal proportion**.

## In the Slice

**Encounter location:** the Thinn inhabit a single gas-rich
hydrogen-helium world ("Spire," a sub-Neptune in the slice cluster)
whose upper atmosphere has the pressure dynamics they require to
fly. Lower atmosphere is too dense; they would lose ribbon-stability.

**Encounter shape:** a polite, contemplative dialog with a Thinn
elder ("We-Who-Witness-the-Edge"). They explain their plan. They
ask the Furling Steward to *not Migrate them* — they would die.
They ask the Furling Steward to *not interfere with their
edge-on suspension*. They thank the Steward for the courtesy of
having asked at all.

**Player choices** (per the slice's Convince/Intimidate/Dominate/
Eliminate/Cloak/Hide framework — canonical FSM in
`tools/quest_inventory.csv`, quest_id `thinn_edge_align`):
- **Honor their plan** — leave them alone; the slice records their
  fate as *Pending* (will they survive? unknown). Aligns Persuader.
- **Insist on Cloak** — install a Cloaking Satellite, suppressing
  their cognition. The Thinn accept reluctantly. Their fate becomes
  *Cloaked*. Aligns Compeller (forced help is still help).
- **Cleanse** — they were doomed anyway; do it cleanly. Aligns
  Cleanser. (This is the canonically darkest option in the slice.
  The Thinn deliver the *"half-gone… punctuation"* line.)
- **Defender / Be-Loud offer** — equip them with Burvixese-Caster-style
  amplifier tech to "be loud" alongside the Burv Caster project.
  They politely refuse — they understand the Burvixese plan and
  consider it (literally) flat-out wrong. (Pun: theirs.)

**Award if visited:** Bio-Archive entry + a unique inventory item:
a single Thinn shed-ribbon (a piece of their molted-edge that
preserves the iridescent shift). Decorative; possibly a Furling
Council gift item in a future increment.

## Ship Design

**The Thinn Blade** (per ship-roster authoring schema; renamed from
*Planar Blade* 2026-05-17).

- **Form**: a flat sail-craft, roughly elliptical, with the Thinn
  pilot embedded along the sail's spine. The ship itself is also
  zero-width-along-one-axis, mirroring its pilots.
- **side**: homesteader
- **points**: ~110 (lower than Furling Scout's 130; the Thinn Blade
  is a glass cannon)
- **hull_max**: 50 (very fragile)
- **shield_max**: 0 (no shields — they don't believe in defending
  what's already invisible enough)
- **top_speed**: 360 (very fast in preferred direction)
- **turn_rate**: 1.5 (slow turn — they're a sail, not an arrow)
- **acceleration**: 200
- **ai_style**: clever_flier (a new style for the AI module — they
  prefer broadside-to-target attacks at long range, then turn
  edge-on between volleys)

**Primary weapon — Thinn Arc.**
A shotgun-like cone of plasma that emerges from the ship's leading
edge. **Wider and weaker the further it travels.** Close range it
is devastating (5+ pellets at full damage); long range it is a
gentle scattering (1 pellet at half damage). Damage rolls off with
distance — the opposite of most beam weapons. Reload is fast.

- primary_damage: 4 per pellet, 6 pellets per shot at close, 1 pellet
  at max range
- primary_energy: 4
- primary_rate: 1.5 shots/sec
- primary_range: 600 (but useful only at <300)
- primary_speed: 800 (slow plasma cloud)

**Special — Edge-Align.**
The Thinn Blade turns instantly edge-on to its current heading,
becoming **immune to primary fire from any ship pointed at it from
directly ahead** for 2 seconds. During Edge-Align it cannot fire and
cannot turn. Costs 30 energy. Cooldown 8 seconds.
- **Tactical wrinkle:** the immunity is from the FRONT only. A
  flanker can still hit them. The Thinn Blade's player/AI must
  consciously face the threat to use Edge-Align defensively, which
  also leaves them unable to retreat directly during the immunity
  window — they must roll out of it before reorienting.
- **Counter:** orbit the Thinn Blade so you're never on its forward
  axis. Or, hit it with a high-projectile-spread weapon that arrives
  from multiple angles simultaneously.

**Lore note on ship colors**: hull_color matches the Thinn's own
iridescence — teal/violet/gold shifting in render based on the
ship's facing angle relative to the camera. (Cosmetic only; doesn't
affect gameplay.)

## What the Thinn Add to the Slice's Thematic Picture

Every species in the slice tests a different theory about how to
survive the Others' arrival. The Thinn are the **geometric
solution** — survive by *being too flat to register*. They are the
non-obvious thematic counterpart to the Slylandro (suppress signal
chemically), Mmrnmhrm (defense doesn't work, accept fate
robotically), Chenjesu (be slow enough that you read as geology),
Burvixese (try to be loud enough that you read as too costly — fails),
Utwig (devolve below threshold via chosen culture), Taalo (build
a shield and witness its failure with dignity), and Mycon (accidentally
awaken into a new signal).

The Thinn take the cognition-detection threshold *literally* —
they reduce their own cross-section to zero. Whether the Others
detect cognition by cross-section or by some other metric is the
unanswered question their fate will settle. **Slice terminal status:
Pending.** The Thinn are the slice's one genuinely-unresolved
Homesteader case by design — the player leaves without knowing if
their geometric solution worked.

## Canon Note — Forward (the Thinn rebel; weapons-officer recruit option)

> **Added 2026-05-17** per Aaron's brief: there is **one** canonical Thinn who has rejected the Edge-Align doctrine and the collective-plural pronouns. His name is **Forward** (single word; refusal of multi-word convention). He is the first Thinn to use singular pronouns in 50,000 years. He left Spire 3 years before slice and has been waiting at Mh-Lai docks for a Steward willing to take him aboard.
>
> **Forward is a recruitable weapons officer** — mutually exclusive with Bren-Vor Telcas (the slot can only hold one). His recruitment requires the Steward to have visited Spire at least once (so they know the species before they meet the rebel). See [crew-recruitment-quests.md](crew-recruitment-quests.md) "Forward — Thinn Rebel" section for full backstory + recruitment + side-quest content.
>
> **His doctrinal disagreement** with the troupe is preserved as canon: the troupe correctly observes that *the Edge-Align doctrine does not require seeing the Others*. Forward responds that *he* requires seeing them. The Thinn cognition-canon (zero brain volume; cannot revise plans) applies to Forward too — *he is also probably wrong* about his Forward doctrine. But he will *see* the predator, which neither he nor the troupe could otherwise. The Steward will get to enjoy his 2D jokes for the rest of the slice if recruited.
>
> **Linguistic heresy**: singular pronouns are *medically inadvisable* for Thinn cognitive substrate (canonical observation by Furling xenophysiologists). Forward's adoption of "I" cost him something — *"some days I think my own thoughts are dimmer for being only mine."* This is the Thinn canonical cost of individuation.
