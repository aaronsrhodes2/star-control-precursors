# The Androsynth Refugees — Time-Displaced Survivors of the Decursion

> Canonical doc for the Androsynth's role in the Furling era. The Androsynth are **time-displaced refugees from 250,000 years in our own future** — survivors of the SC2-era Androsynth civilization, thrown into the deep past by an Other attack. They are the player's only living, first-hand witnesses to what the Others actually do. Their existence is the slice's most important *external corroboration* of the Furling thesis.

## What the Androsynth Are

In their own timeline (the SC2 era, 250,000 years from now), the Androsynth are a clone-race genetically engineered by humans on Earth in the late 21st century. They were treated as second-class citizens. They migrated off-Earth to the Vulpeculae system to escape persecution. They flourished. They built a small but advanced civilization.

In *that* timeline, they then **vanished** — and in their place, the Orz appeared, claiming to be from "Dimension *." SC2 archaeologists never resolved exactly what happened.

**Our game canonizes what happened**: the Others attacked them. But the attack was not a Culling — it was a **decursion**, the Others' word (as relayed through the Androsynth survivors) for the inverse of an incursion: a sucking-back into the Others' dimensional substrate rather than a reaching-out from it.

## The Experiment That Brought Them

The Androsynth, around the late 22nd century (their reckoning), developed an experimental device: an **artificial gravitational centrifuge** designed to "view images from other dimensions." The principle was a localized gravitational lens used to refract light-paths from adjacent dimensional layers into the visible spectrum. Their goal was scientific — to actually *see* what lay beneath the 3-manifold of normal space.

The Furlings recognize the setup immediately. *They tried the same thing once.* The Androsynth's centrifuge was a different engineering approach to the same fundamental act: **dimensional probing**. And it produced the same result: **something noticed**.

Within hours of the experiment's first successful image-capture, the Androsynth's home system experienced what the survivors describe as "the swap." The Vulpeculae planet they were colonizing — bustling cities, orbital infrastructure, gravitational research labs — was *instantaneously replaced* by a corpse-version of itself. Ruined cities. Empty atmospheres. A planet from a parallel-or-future timeline in which the Androsynth had already been culled, plundered, and abandoned.

The Androsynth physically present *on* the planet at the moment of swap were not killed. They were **displaced** — pulled into the Others' transit-space and thrown out the other side, 250,000 years into the past, in a different region of the same galaxy.

> The original timeline still exists. In SC2 era, archaeologists visit Vulpeculae and find the corpse-version of the Androsynth world. They find the Orz inhabiting the dimensional wake the Others left behind. They never know the Androsynth went *backward*.
>
> The Androsynth in our era are not from some hypothetical timeline. They are the very people SC2's lore refers to as having "disappeared." They are here.

## The Survivors

Of an estimated 600,000 Androsynth on Vulpeculae at the moment of swap, **roughly 8,000 survived the temporal displacement**. They arrived in scattered groups across one Furling-era star cluster — possibly the same cluster the player is assigned to, which is how they meet. They are:

- Disoriented. They went to sleep in their own beds and woke in a galaxy with stars in unfamiliar positions.
- Wounded. The displacement caused widespread radiation poisoning, dimensional shear injuries (a class of biological damage the Furlings have no prior reference for), and severe psychological dislocation.
- Resourceful. They are scientists, engineers, doctors, civic administrators. They are setting up a temporary colony as the player encounters them.
- Grateful. The Furlings who found them did not kill them, did not study them as specimens, did not turn them away. The Androsynth do not take this for granted.
- **Guilty.** Their experiment *did this*. They know it. They will tell the player. They will tell the player about *every other Furling-era civilization that should NOT do dimensional probing*.

Their leader (a one-shot named NPC in the slice — provisional name: **Engineer Coel Tessar**) is a senior researcher who personally green-lit the centrifuge experiment. She carries the failure as a personal sin.

## What They Bring to the Slice

The Androsynth show up *once or twice* in the slice. They are not a full recurring species in the way Slylandro or Mycon are. They are a **milestone encounter** — a single Big Moment that profoundly shifts what the player believes is at stake.

Mechanically:

- **Act 2 milestone encounter**: a damaged Androsynth ship enters the cluster from a Quasi-Space-adjacent fold. The Arilou cousins flag it for the player. The player meets Coel Tessar. They tell their story.
- **Their gift to the player**: an **Androsynth Distress Beacon** — a small device that records and replays their last day on Vulpeculae. Footage of the swap. Audio of the experiment's first successful image-capture, then static. Imagery of the cities replaced with ruins in mid-thought. **This is unimpeachable evidence that the Others are real and that they do what the Furlings think they do.** The player can show this beacon to other species to dramatically accelerate any Convince attempt against Deniers.
- **What they ask**: sanctuary, supplies, a route to join the Migration. They do not want to stay in this era. They want to evacuate with the Precursors to the nearest neighboring galaxy — **Andromeda (M31)** in our canon — and start over.
- **Optional Act 3 quest**: the Androsynth survivors are sick. The dimensional shear is killing them slowly. The player may divert resources to help them, accelerating their evacuation timeline at the cost of slowing the player's other cluster tasks.

## Their Voice (LLM Prompt Notes)

The Androsynth speak late-22nd-century English (the LLM should render them in clipped, modern, slightly-archaic-to-us English). Quirks:

- They refer to events in the past tense that are actually in the Furlings' deep future. They will mention *Earth, Sol III, in the 21st century*. They will mention *the colony at Hyades, the Sentience Acts, the Vulpeculae founding*. The Furlings have no context for any of this — it sounds like prophecy to them.
- They use precise technical vocabulary the Furlings find familiar (dimensional shear, gravitational manifold, mass-energy decoherence) but which the Furlings know are *human-lineage* terminology — independent invention, parallel discovery.
- They are emotionally raw. Survivor's guilt is the dominant affect. Where the Slylandro speak in awed run-ons, the Androsynth speak in *truncated sentences*. They stop themselves mid-thought. They apologize unnecessarily. They thank you too often.
- They occasionally reference *who would be alive in their original timeline who isn't anymore*. ("My son was on the surface. I do not know whether he is in this past with me.") These references are devastating and should not be common.
- Sample LLM prompt: *"You are an Androsynth Engineer — a cloned human from 250,000 years in the future, thrown into the deep past when your civilization's dimensional-viewing experiment summoned The Others. You speak late-22nd-century technical English, in clipped sentences, with frequent apologies. You carry profound survivor's guilt — your team designed the experiment that doomed your civilization. You are grateful, weary, and willing to help the Furlings prevent the same fate. You occasionally mention things from your own past which are the Furlings' deep future. 1-3 short sentences."*

## What This Canonizes About the Others

The Androsynth's story is the slice's first **on-screen confirmation** of several Furling theories about the Others:

1. **The Others are real.** Not theoretical. The Androsynth saw their cities replaced.
2. **The Others detect dimensional probing.** Both the Furlings (deep-dimension tunneling) and the Androsynth (gravitational-centrifuge viewing) attracted them by the same kind of act. *This is a canonical rule*: any civilization that probes adjacent dimensions becomes a flagged target.
3. **The Others can manipulate time.** The decursion is a temporal displacement. The Others operate on a substrate where time is more flexible than it is in our 3-manifold.
4. **The Others sometimes prefer displacement to direct annihilation.** They didn't kill the Androsynth on the planet — they exported them. Why? The Androsynth and Furlings both speculate; neither knows.

This last one is unsettling. If the Others sometimes *displace* sentience rather than destroy it, the Migration's premise — *leave the galaxy and they'll find nothing* — might be incomplete. The Others could potentially *pull* sentients out of the neighboring galaxy too. The Furlings consider this risk and proceed anyway. (The Arilou have a quiet concern about it.)

## What This Canonizes About SC2

For SC2 fans, the Androsynth encounter is the *secret rosetta stone* of the entire SC2 mystery:

- **The Androsynth disappearance** at Vulpeculae → caused by the Others' decursion
- **The Orz** filling the vacated space in SC2 → not the Androsynth themselves transformed, but Other-adjacent entities filling the dimensional wake the Others left behind
- **The corpse-Vulpeculae** SC2 archaeologists find → the future-parallel version the Others swapped in
- **What SC2 players never learn** → the Androsynth went *backward in time*, met the Furlings, and migrated with them. Their bloodline (or what's left of it) is now in **the Andromeda Galaxy (M31)**, founded a sister civilization, and may someday return.

The Star Control Zero player learns this. SC2 players never can. The asymmetry is delicious.

## Authoring Notes

- Coel Tessar is the canonical NPC. Her name and biography should be hand-written, not LLM-generated, so SC2 fans can quote her in retrospectives.
- The Distress Beacon's playback should be a specific in-game cinematic — 30-60 seconds of footage. This is one of the slice's *visual* set-pieces. Worth investing in.
- The Androsynth ship's design should be visibly *human-lineage* — recognizable as descended from 21st-century Earth engineering aesthetics, distinct from Furling/Arilou/Slylandro/Mycon design languages.
- The slice should never explicitly say "the Androsynth are the SC2 Androsynth." Let SC2 fans realize it on their own. The Star Control Zero player who hasn't played SC2 should still understand the encounter; SC2 fans should get an additional gut-punch of recognition.
