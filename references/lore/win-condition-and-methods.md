# Win Condition and the Methods Toolkit

> Canon for the game's victory criterion and the toolkit of tactics the player can apply to each species. Companion to [the-precursors-and-homesteaders.md](the-precursors-and-homesteaders.md) (faction structure) and [furling-tech-mechanics.md](furling-tech-mechanics.md) (the Time Drive that makes per-encounter failure recoverable).

## The Win Condition

> **You win by handling every sentient species in the galaxy before the Migration window closes, then crossing into the nearest neighboring galaxy yourself — leaving what remains to the Others, hoping one day to return.**

"Handling" a species means resolving its status into one of these terminal states:

| Terminal status | What it means | Detectability |
|---|---|---|
| **Migrated** | The species crossed the dimensional crossing into the nearest neighboring galaxy | Gone — emissions zero |
| **Cloaked** | The species remains in our galaxy but is below the Others' detection threshold (e.g. Slylandro Cloaking Satellite) | Below threshold |
| **Hidden** (Arilou path) | The species followed the Arilou into Quasi-Space exile | Adjacent dimension; effectively undetectable |
| **Eliminated** | The species is dead | Zero |
| **Pre-sentient** (irrelevant) | Never developed detectable thought patterns; the Others can't perceive them | Below threshold by biology |

The galaxy wins quiet when **every species is in one of these states**. The player Steward's job, cluster by cluster, is to move the species in their area through to one of those states. The full game tracks a **Galaxy Status** board listing every sentient civilization and its terminal status; the slice tracks the same thing for its 4 species.

### The lose condition (sort of)

There is no traditional "Game Over." The Time Drive handles individual catastrophes (combat death, bad call). The bigger failure modes are:

- **Miss the Migration deadline** with an unhandled sentient civilization still emitting. The Others arrive. The cluster (or the galaxy) is culled. *The player can still escape* (cross with the Migration that's leaving anyway), but the species you failed are dead. The HUD will tell you this in the ending text.
- **Refuse the Migration entirely** (Defender path). You stay with the Homesteaders. Your slice ends with you watching the Migration leave. This is canonically the *closest path to SC2 lore* — most of the SC2-era surviving species are descendants of Homesteader paths in clusters like yours. You don't "lose"; you just took the path that becomes the SC2 universe.

### "Hoping one day to return"

The win condition includes the *hope* that the migrated Precursors can eventually return to the regrowing galaxy. The Furlings believe the Culling has a duration. They believe the Others will move on. They believe the galaxy will be habitable again. They are not *certain*. The final scene of the slice is the player crossing the threshold — and the tone is hope, not triumph.

## The Methods Toolkit

For each sentient species you encounter, you have a toolkit of methods. Different methods suit different species, different situations, and different faction alignments. The player's Council standing shifts based on which methods they use most.

### Method 1 — Convince

*Diplomatic persuasion.* Talk to the species' leadership, explain the Others, walk them through the Migration option, give them time to consult their elders / governments / philosophers. The Slylandro need centuries; you have decades. **Persuader-faction doctrine.**

- **Slow** — multiple visits over in-game time
- **High species trust** afterward
- **No moral cost** — species made an informed choice
- **Fails** when the species is biologically rooted (can't migrate even if convinced), when communication is impossible (proto-Ur-Quan), or when time runs out

### Method 2 — Intimidate

*Show of force without harm.* Bring a fleet. Demonstrate Furling weapons against an asteroid in their system. Speak in low tones about what happens if they're still here when the Others arrive. Make compliance the obviously-rational choice. **Compeller-doctrine, mild variant.**

- **Faster than Convince**
- **Damages species trust permanently**
- **No deaths but the species remembers you with fear**
- **Effective** against pragmatic species or fractious civilizations who'll fold under pressure
- **Backfires** on honor-bound species (Yehat ancestors might double down) and against species the Arilou are watching closely (they disapprove)

### Method 3 — Dominate

*Force compliance.* Cleanser-and-Compeller hybrid: actually use Furling military or biological tech to extract them against their will. Sedate, ferry through the crossing, install cloaks on planets without permission, replace governments with cooperative ones. **Compeller-doctrine, hard variant.**

- **Fast**
- **Destroys species trust**
- **No deaths but profound moral cost**
- **Effective on small or politically-fragmented species**
- **Forbidden** by Persuader-faction sanction; the Council formally censures repeat use even though some Compellers do it anyway
- **Note**: the Arilou will withdraw assistance from Stewards who Dominate routinely

### Method 4 — Eliminate

*Kill them.* **Cleanser doctrine.** Quiet weapons, engineered plagues, stellar manipulation to render a system uninhabitable. The species' emission goes to zero permanently. The Quiet is restored. The Cleansers consider this the deepest mercy in the entire plan. The Furling Council formally records each Eliminated species in the **Quiet Ledger**.

- **Fastest**
- **No species trust to damage; the species is gone**
- **Profound moral weight**
- **Permanent and irreversible** — Time Drive cannot rewind a *Quiet Ledger entry* (this is one of the canonical irreversible story flags)
- **Effective when nothing else will work in time**

### Alternative — Cloak

Not a method *against* the species but a method *for* them. Build them a Cloaking Satellite (or equivalent dampener) so they can stay in their home but fall below the Others' detection threshold. The Slylandro path. Requires:

- Species' consent (or Domination to install without it)
- A Furling engineering build (expensive in time and antimatter)
- The species must be **biologically locatable** (i.e. they're attached to a single planet/system that can be cloaked)

### Alternative — Hide (Arilou path)

The species joins the Arilou in voluntary Quasi-Space exile. Rare; only viable for species the Arilou will accept (close genetic or cultural cousins, or species that have earned Arilou trust). Effectively the same outcome as Migrate from the Others' perspective — they're undetectable — but the species stays *adjacent* to our galaxy rather than crossing.

## Per-Species Method Viability

For the slice's four species:

| Species | Convince | Intimidate | Dominate | Eliminate | Cloak | Hide |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Slylandro** | ✅ slow but trustful | ❌ they will simply grieve and not change | ⚠️ possible but they cannot physically migrate; you'd dominate them into accepting a cloak | ✅ Cleanser doctrine | ✅ **The canonical Slylandro path — the Satellite** | ❌ Arilou won't accept gas-giant atmospheric sentience |
| **Proto-Ur-Quan** | n/a — non-verbal | n/a | ⚠️ uplift-then-evacuate (severe) | n/a — won't help, they're non-detectable | n/a | n/a |
| **Mycon biots** (non-sentient) | n/a — pre-language | n/a | n/a | ⚠️ Cleanser pre-emptive (kill before Deep Child awakens) | ⚠️ unusual: cloak the biot hive itself | n/a |
| **Mycon biots** (post–Deep Child awakening) | ⚠️ possible if you treat the Deep Child as legitimate dialogue | ⚠️ they may not fear death | ⚠️ rooted to mantle, cannot migrate | ✅ Cleanser doctrine (most-used) | ✅ expensive — install a planetary-scale cloak | ⚠️ rare — Arilou might accept fungal sentience |
| **Arilou** | n/a — they've already chosen their path | n/a — they're more powerful than your fleet | n/a | n/a | n/a | ✅ — *they invented this path* |

The proto-Ur-Quan row's recurring "n/a" is the *core mercy* of their situation: they're invisible to the Others, so no method needs to apply. **The merciful action is inaction.**

## Method Usage Tracking

The game tracks per-method usage at the Steward level. This feeds into:

- **Council standing** with each Furling method-faction (Persuader / Compeller / Cleanser). Heavy Convince usage → Persuader friendship; heavy Eliminate → Cleanser commendation; heavy Dominate → Compeller alignment but Persuader / Arilou disapproval.
- **Arilou trust** specifically — they reward Convince, tolerate Cloak, disapprove of Dominate, withdraw entirely from Eliminate (they won't deal with you after one Eliminate, but they'll mourn with you over a forced one).
- **Slice endings** reflect what you actually did. The four canonical endings (Persuader / Compeller / Cleanser / Defender) map to "your dominant method, weighted toward severity."

## The Cluster Status Board (slice UI)

The slice needs a small UI showing the cluster's species and their handled/unhandled status:

```
═══ CLUSTER STATUS ═══
Slylandro Observers      [ Cloaking Satellite installed — terminal: CLOAKED ]
Mycon Biot Hive          [ Deep Child suppressed — terminal: PRE-SENTIENT  ]
Proto-Ur-Quan colonies   [ left undisturbed — terminal: PRE-SENTIENT       ]
Arilou Outpost           [ withdrawing to exile — terminal: HIDDEN          ]

Migration deadline:  T-minus 4 years, 8 months
Council standing:    Persuaders favor you, Cleansers disappointed
```

When all species in the cluster are in terminal status and the Rainbow World is seeded, the slice ending unlocks. The full game's Galaxy Status board is the same UI scaled to ~40 sentient civilizations across all clusters.

## Two Reasons Methods Matter (the deeper design)

1. **They reveal who the player is.** A Convince-first Steward and an Eliminate-first Steward play the same game with the same balance, but they live in very different moral spaces. The slice's epilogue tells them what they did.

2. **They feed the LLM dialog system with concrete history.** When an Arilou meets you, the LLM's prompt includes "this Steward has Eliminated two species and Convinced none." The Arilou's dialog reflects that. The variation principle makes this *show* — the third Arilou you meet remembers what the first one knew.

## What This Doesn't Add

- A "score." There is no point total. The game tracks species statuses and faction standing, not abstract points.
- A "kill counter." Each Eliminate is named. The Quiet Ledger preserves the names of the species you removed. There is no anonymizing aggregation.
- A "best ending." The Persuader path is canonically lauded but the slice doesn't *call it* the best. All four canonical endings are valid; SC2 canon emerges from the Defender path.
