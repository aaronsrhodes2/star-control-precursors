# The Precursors and the Homesteaders — Canonical Faction Split

> **Authority**: this document is canon for the high-level factional structure of the Furling-era debate. It supersedes the more granular faction list in [factions-and-war.md](factions-and-war.md), which now lives as *sub-positions* under the Stay/Go umbrella defined here.

## The Two Sides — Stays and Goes

As the Migration debate hardens, the galaxy's sentient civilizations settle into two named factions. Sub-positions exist within each (different reasons for staying, different methods for going), but the binary is the politically operative split.

### The Homesteaders — *The Stays*

The species and Furling sub-cultures who refuse to leave the galaxy. They call themselves **Homesteaders**. The name implies what binds them: *this is home, we are not leaving home*. They are heterogeneous in their reasoning:

- **Deniers** — don't believe the Others are real, or coming, or threatening
- **Defenders** — believe the Others can be fought (the Sa-Matra program belongs to them)
- **Skeptics** — believe in the Others but distrust the Furling-led plan
- **The Rooted** — *cannot* leave biologically. The Mycon biots' mycelial spore-networks tie them to specific planetary mantles; uprooting kills them. (The proto-Mycon are *the* exemplar Homesteaders by necessity, not choice — they have no other option.)
- **The Honorable** — believe life away from home is a fate worse than death; would rather die in their home than live elsewhere
- **The Cynics** — believe the Furlings are using the Migration to consolidate power in the new galaxy at the expense of those who stay

The Homesteaders are NOT a monolith. Some will fight Furlings who try to extract them; some will simply refuse and pray. Some will join the Defender military; some will quietly build deep-rock shelters. The unifying thread is *not leaving*.

### The Precursors — *The Goes*

The species and Furling sub-cultures who agree to the Migration and travel through the Rainbow Worlds' dimensional crossing to the nearest neighboring galaxy — canonically **the Andromeda Galaxy (M31)**, our nearest large spiral neighbor. They call themselves **the Precursors** — *those who go before*. In their own usage, the name means: *we go ahead of the regrowing galaxy, we precede whatever new sentience will arise after the Culling, we are the keepers of the seed-civilization in the new galaxy until it is safe to return*.

The Precursors include:

- The Furlings as a civilization (led by their Persuaders, supported by Compellers and Cleansers)
- Species who believe the Furlings about the Others
- Species who think their homeworlds will survive their absence (they intend to return)
- Species who have already lost incursions to the Others elsewhere and know what's coming
- Species who don't think they can win a fight
- Species whose biology *allows* migration (most of them)

> ## The Great Misinterpretation
>
> Two hundred and fifty thousand years later, SC2-era observers will dig up ruins, decode Furling artifacts, and find references to "the Precursors" everywhere. They will assume this name refers to *the Furlings themselves* — the shaggy-giant civilization whose tech is scattered across the galaxy. **They are wrong.** "Precursors" was a *faction name* used in the final centuries before the Migration, referring to *all the species that left together*, of which the Furlings were one (the most prominent). The misinterpretation is understandable: the Furlings led the faction and their tech is the most visible, so the name attached to them in retrospect. But canonically — *in our game* — the Precursors are *the departing faction* and the Furlings call themselves Furlings.
>
> This is why the game's *title* is "Star Control Zero: **The Precursors**" and yet the player character is a **Furling**. Both are correct. The title refers to the faction the player belongs to (and the future-misinterpretation it will inspire); the in-character name is Furling.

## The Third Path — The Arilou's Voluntary Exile

The Arilou Lalee'lay are neither Homesteaders nor (quite) Precursors. They invented a third option that the Furlings considered too sacrificial to ask of anyone else:

**The Arilou are staying in Quasi-Space to watch over the regrowing galaxy as the Others pass through.**

They are *aligned* with the Precursor faction — they believe in the Migration, they support the evacuation, they help the Furlings — but they are not going to the nearest neighboring galaxy. They are hiding in the folded layers of Quasi-Space adjacent to our 3D galaxy, where the Others' detection apparatus cannot easily reach. From there, for tens of thousands of years, they will watch what happens after the Furlings leave: which Homesteaders survived, which awoke, which were culled. They will witness the long slow regrowth of life. They will be there to greet the Newcomers when they arise.

The Arilou call this strategy *the long silence-watching*. It is voluntary, it is sacrificial — they will be alone in a Quasi-Space pocket for geological time — and it is informed by their unique trans-temporal cognition (they can perceive the bleed of moments adjacent to the present, so the loneliness is differently shaped than it would be for a Furling).

The Furlings considered asking other species to join the Arilou. Most refused. A small number of Furling Hider-faction researchers may have joined the Arilou in Quasi-Space; this is unconfirmed canon, available for future story development.

## The Slylandro Cloaking Satellite — The Cloak That Worked

The Slylandro are the slice's exemplar of *a Homesteader species that survived*. The Slylandro live in the upper atmosphere of a single gas giant in Beta Corvi. Sentient, slow, deeply attached to their home. They cannot survive leaving their gas giant (their physiology is the gas currents of that planet; they are not transportable). Migration is impossible for them.

The Furlings developed and deployed the **Slylandro Cloaking Satellite** — a self-maintaining orbital construct around the Slylandro gas giant that dampens the Slylandro's thought-pattern emissions to below the Others' detection threshold. The Slylandro are *cognitively alive* but appear, to the Others, *as quiet as a methane storm*. The satellite is a high-end Furling engineering achievement and burns enormous amounts of antimatter; it is built to last 100,000+ years on a single charge.

**It works.** In SC2 era, the Slylandro are still alive in Beta Corvi, still tending their atmosphere, still observing newcomers — *because the cloaking satellite is still operating, 250,000 years later*. SC2 players who visit them in Beta Corvi are talking to a species that survived the Culling because of a piece of Furling tech the player Steward helped install in the slice we are designing.

This is a *concrete in-game artifact*. The player builds it (or chooses not to) in the cluster's late-Act-3 / Act-4 task tree.

## The Others' Detection Mechanism

The Others detect prey via **complex thought patterns at great distance**. This is the canonical mechanism, never to be contradicted by future design. Implications:

- Bacteria, plants, animals → invisible. Pre-sentient mollusc colonies → invisible. The proto-Ur-Quan are *safe* not because they're hidden but because their cognition isn't complex enough to ping the detector.
- Single-species civilizations → easily detected at distance.
- Concentrations of multiple sentient civilizations → flares the detector across light-years.
- Cognitive enhancement, AI infrastructure, mass telepathy → all amplify the signal.
- *Dampening* (cloaking satellites, dimensional folding, etc.) can reduce the signature. This is the basis of the Slylandro Satellite and the Arilou Quasi-Space hide.
- The Others' detection threshold is uncertain — the Furlings think they know it, but the threshold could be lower than they realize. Players will not know either; this ambiguity is part of the slice's tension.

**Gameplay consequence**: any in-game system that increases or decreases a species' "thought-pattern signature" matters. The Mycon biots' awakening into Deep Child sentience is *literally an emission spike the Others might detect from the next cluster over*. This is why the Cleansers want them suppressed: a single biot waking up wrong could be the beacon that brings the Others a year early.

## Mapping Old Furling Factions to Stay/Go

The six Furling factions from [factions-and-war.md](factions-and-war.md) map onto the broader Stay/Go split:

| Old faction | New alignment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Persuaders | Precursor | Evacuate peacefully |
| Compellers | Precursor | Evacuate by sedation/deception if needed |
| Cleansers | Precursor (extreme) | Euthanize Homesteaders so the Quiet succeeds — they consider themselves the cost of Migration |
| Defenders | Homesteader | Refuse to flee; build the Sa-Matra; stay and fight |
| Deniers | Homesteader | Reject the threat |
| Hiders | **Split** — some Precursor-aligned (build cloaking tech like the Slylandro satellite then leave with the Migration), some Homesteader-aligned (build cloaking enclaves and stay in them), some Arilou-aligned (join the Arilou in Quasi-Space) |

The split within the Hiders is the most interesting — they're the engineering faction, and their tech (cloaks, dimensional folds) gets used by both sides.

## Slice Task Tree (Updated)

The slice's late acts now have a more nuanced task tree, reflecting the player's choice of path:

- **Act 3 — The Cleanser Arrives.** Same as before. Player chooses how to handle the Slylandro question.
- **Act 4 — The Tasks.** Depending on Act 3:
  - **If Slylandro will migrate** (Persuader/Compeller paths): seed the cluster's Rainbow World; usher the Slylandro through the crossing.
  - **If Slylandro will stay** (Hider/Defender paths): install the **Slylandro Cloaking Satellite**; seed the cluster's Rainbow World *anyway* (the Migration needs all 10 worlds regardless of any single cluster's species choices).
  - **If Slylandro are euthanized** (Cleanser path): seed the cluster's Rainbow World; the cluster's task tree is shorter; the slice ends in the Quiet Ledger.
- **Act 4 ending — The Door.** Player chooses whether to:
  - Cross with the Migration (join the Precursors in the nearest neighboring galaxy)
  - Stay as a Watcher in the bereft galaxy (a small number of Furlings did this — they become legend)
  - Join the Arilou in Quasi-Space (rare, requires high Arilou trust)
  - Stand with the Homesteaders (Defender path; the closest path to SC2 canon's surviving species)

The Rainbow World seeding is *always* an act-4 task (the broader Migration needs it); the Slylandro Cloak is conditional on Slylandro fate; the personal ending is the player's choice independent of those tasks.

## What This Means for the Game's Title

*"Star Control Zero: The Precursors"* — the title now has two correct readings:

1. (External, the marketing reading): "The Precursors" as SC2 fans understand the word — the shaggy-giant civilization whose ruins shape the galaxy. The game is the *story of what they did*.
2. (Internal, the canonical reading): "The Precursors" as a faction the player can choose to join. The game is the *story of the choice to go*.

Both work. Both should be readable from the title. The player learns the second meaning over the course of play.

## Anti-Patterns (do not break)

- Do NOT have any in-fiction character use the word "Precursor" to mean "the Furlings as a species." The word always refers to the migration faction. Confusion will be reserved for SC2-era observers.
- Do NOT let the Arilou be casual about their choice. Voluntary geological-time isolation is profoundly heavy; their dialog should reflect it (the existing multi-tense affect already supports this — their "we were-are-will-be" grammar can read as the speech of someone consulting a long lonely timeline they're about to live).
- Do NOT let the Slylandro satellite be a *small* artifact. It is a major Furling engineering achievement. The seeding-of-cloak is a Big Moment, mechanically and visually.
- Do NOT contradict the Others' detection mechanism. Anything that adds gameplay around stealth/exposure must respect "complex thought patterns at distance."
