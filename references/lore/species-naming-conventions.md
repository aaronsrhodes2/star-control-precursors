# Species Naming Conventions — Cultural-Anchor Bible

> Authored 2026-05-17 from Aaron's canon: *"Each species needs a naming convention that follows what that culture values most. Like the Melnorme name their ships all after shipping phrases like 'The Going-out-of-business Sale' or 'The Dollar General'. All jokes or puns. The Burvixese are all about loudness so it's all about the volume — 'The Long-term Hearing Damage' and 'All About the Bass' might be names for their ships."*

This doc canonizes how every species in the slice names its **ships, installations, weapons, artifacts, and noteworthy individuals**. The naming convention is *derived from the species' cultural value-anchor* — what that culture cares most about, expressed through every named thing that culture produces.

## Why this matters

In SC2, every ship the player encounters has a unique instance name. The Ur-Quan Dreadnought is *"The Sa-Matra of Vorndar"*; the Melnorme trader is *"The Slow Spinneret"*. These names carry character — they tell you who the species is before they speak. Our slice extends this to every culturally-coherent named thing:

- **Ship instances** in hyperspace + super-melee + cinematic combat
- **Named installations** (the Caster, the Shield, the Vault)
- **Stations + outposts** (Mh-Lai Station, Hearth-of-Iron, Furlmart)
- **Weapons + artifacts** (the Decursion, the Distress Beacon, the Resonance Record)
- **Notable individuals** (Sentinel-Aux Theta-Four, Veiled-In-Three-Days, Foreman Vesh-Kar Twel-Pin)

The naming convention is one of the slice's *primary character vectors*. A player who's seen four Melnorme ships should be able to predict the fifth's name's *shape* — and laugh when they read it.

### Dnyarri — *Hidden, parasitic, host-names*

Anchor: they have no ship of their own. They name the *host* ship by its host species' convention, sometimes with subtle off-notes a perceptive Steward might catch. The Dnyarri pilot is **invisible by design**; the name reads as a normal Cleanser or Mycon or Melnorme name, with one word *slightly* wrong. (Aaron's canon: the Dnyarri make their disguise look like any catalog ship; the reveal is at first fire.) When the Dnyarri *is* revealed, the floating tag reads `"DNYARRI — controlling [HOST_NAME]"`.

Per the Dnyarri Disguised Encounter mechanic, the canonical reveal-tag format is: `DNYARRI — controlling [host species' instance name]`. The host-name is the cover's actual sampled name from the host species' bank.

### Proto-species — *No names yet*

Pre-sentient species (proto-Ur-Quan, proto-Shofixti, proto-Spathi, etc.) have no naming convention because they have no names — they have not yet self-distinguished. The Furlings refer to them by location (*"the Spathi-precursor population at Sigma Tauri"*).

### The Others — *No translation, no names*

The Others do not name their vessel. The Furlings call it "the Others' Vessel" because the species has no Furling-translatable word for *self*. The reveal-tag in combat just reads `OTHERS`. (Per canon: sameness is part of the horror.)

## Implementation contract

- Code-side registry: `src/scz/content/species_names.py` — `SHIP_NAME_BANKS: dict[species_id, list[str]]`
- Sampling: deterministic-per-encounter via the encounter id as seed (so a repeated encounter has a repeated name; player learns to recognize *specific ships*)
- Display: combat HUD shows the **instance name** rather than the ship-class name; super-melee picker shows the instance name for AI-controlled ships; hyperspace encounter labels can sample the bank for "you see a Cleanser approaching — *The Last Verse*"
- Variation layer (Phase 3 polish): LLM-generated names sampled from the bank's *cultural pattern* rather than the fixed list, for unlimited variety

## Authoring contract

When adding a new species:

1. Identify their cultural value-anchor in one phrase (Burvixese = volume; Melnorme = trade; Utwig = ritual)
2. Write 10-15 example names that obey the convention. Include both stately and absurd entries
3. Add them to the bank in `species_names.py`
4. Update this doc with the canonical entry
5. If the species has named individuals (Foremen, Elders, etc.), the individual naming convention is part of the bank: include both ship-style names AND individual-style names if the species uses different shapes for each
