# The Cleansers — The Ice-Branch Furlings

> **Canon update (2026-05-17, Aaron dispatch):** *"The Cleansers are an ice-centric branch of the Furlings. White fur. Aggressive stubbornness. No empathy."*
>
> This doc canonizes the Cleansers as a biologically distinct **sub-branch of the Furling species**, not merely an ideological faction. The faction's doctrine emerged from the biology; the biology shapes the doctrine. Where the Cleanser-encounter design ([cleanser-encounter-design.md](cleanser-encounter-design.md)) shows them as "gentle, sorrowful, certain," that gentle sorrow is now revealed as a **performed cultural discipline** layered over a substrate that does not natively feel it. The performance is sincere. The biology is colder.
>
> Replaces / supersedes the implicit assumption in [factions-and-war.md](factions-and-war.md) that every Furling has the same baseline empathic capacity and the Cleanser doctrine is a pure act of will against natural feeling. The doctrine is still an act of will — but the will is recruiting a colder substrate to a task the warm-substrate majority could not stomach.

---

## 1. The Canon

> **Three-pole biological symmetry note (2026-05-17 update)**: with the canonization of the [Preservers as the tree-branch](preservers-as-tree-branch.md), the Furling species now has three biological sub-branches mirroring three doctrinal poles: **Ice-branch (Cleansers)** — no empathy; vote to euthanize. **Warm-world (baseline majority)** — baseline empathy; the Furling cultural mainstream. **Tree-branch (Preservers)** — amplified empathy; refuse to harm; vote to preserve. The Cleansers and Preservers are biological *and* doctrinal opposites; the warm-world Furlings are the cultural majority between them. This doc focuses on the ice-branch; the tree-branch doc handles its counterpart.

The Cleansers are an **ice-centric branch** of the Furling species. They are not a separate species; they interbreed with the rest of the Furling population, they share the same Council seats, they speak the same language. But they descend from a Furling lineage that adapted, deep in the species' early history, to cold-world environments — and the adaptations went bone-deep.

Three observable differences from the warm-world Furling baseline:

1. **White fur** — instead of the warm gold-russet-tawny range of the standard Furling phenotype, Cleansers carry a dense white double-coat with faint silver guard-hairs. Their cubs are born white and stay white. Cross-breeds with warm-world Furlings produce mixed coats (canonical: salt-and-pepper, or piebald) which can carry either the cold-world genetic load or the warm-world one in unpredictable proportion. (See *Mixed-Coats* below.)

2. **Aggressive stubbornness** — once a Cleanser has committed to a decision, the cognitive cost of reversing it is unusually high. Other Furlings flex, deliberate, revisit. Cleansers commit and execute. This is congruent with their cold-world ancestral environment: in ice climates, a hunt abandoned mid-pursuit is a hunt that wastes calories the next storm will charge for. The lineage rewarded commitment.

3. **No empathy** — the canonical phrasing. The Cleansers' affective-mirroring substrate is reduced or absent. They cognitively understand that another being is suffering; they do not *feel* the suffering themselves. They can imagine the suffering precisely (their cognitive sophistication is otherwise Furling-baseline) but the imagination does not generate the somatic resonance other Furlings experience. They are not sociopaths in the human sense — they retain duty, honor, tribal loyalty, and a deep ethical framework — but they do not flinch when the math says to act.

The phrasing **"no empathy"** is the Cleansers' own canonical self-description. Other Furlings sometimes soften it ("limited empathic resonance," "the cold-mirror lineage") but the Cleansers themselves say "no empathy" plainly when asked. They consider it a feature of their inheritance, not a defect.

## 2. Why ice-centric and "no empathy" go together (the biology)

Empathy in mammalian-analog species is a metabolic luxury. The affective-mirroring substrate consumes cognitive resources continuously; it produces useful social outputs (alloparenting, alliance, conflict-mediation) but at a calorie cost. In abundant environments, the cost is repaid many times over. In cold-world environments where calories are scarce and predation pressure is high, the cost can outrun the return — and lineages that reduced the substrate gained a survival edge.

The ice-branch Furlings ancestrally hunted on glacier-margins and froze-over inland seas, alone or in small kin-pods. They could not afford to feel for a wounded pack-mate they had decided to abandon, nor for prey that screamed in the trap. The substrate that would have produced that feeling cost them too much. Over millennia of selection, the substrate thinned. The behavior that resulted — purposive, committed, cold — became the lineage's cultural identity as well as its biology.

When the warm-world Furlings eventually contacted the ice-branch (deep prehistory in our era's reckoning), they were horrified at first. They came to appreciate the cold-branch's reliability. The two lineages merged politically over centuries; the biological merge took longer, and is still in progress (the warm-world majority still outnumbers the cold-world minority roughly fifty to one). They are one species now, by every formal measure. They are also, in this one trait, two species in one.

## 3. White fur — visual canon

| Trait | Warm-world Furling (baseline) | Cleanser (ice-branch) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary fur color | Gold-russet, tawny, ochre, copper, dark-honey | Bright white, occasionally with faint silver guard-hairs |
| Undercoat | Single-layer, seasonal molt | Dense double-coat year-round (an ancestral cold-world feature retained) |
| Eye color | Amber, hazel, dark-brown | Pale grey, pale blue, sometimes near-colorless (an ice-glare adaptation) |
| Ear-tuft shape | Pointed, mobile | Rounded, less mobile (heat-conservation; smaller surface area) |
| Average stature | 5-8m (slice-canonical Furling range) | Tends to the upper end (6-8m); slightly stockier; broader shoulders |
| Voice register | Wide pitch range, expressive | Narrower pitch range, flatter cadence; *not* by training — by anatomy. Their vocal apparatus has slightly less modulation. The "gentle sorrow" register they perform in mourning rites is at the limit of their natural expressive range. |

**Mixed-coats**: cross-lineage Furlings (one ice-branch parent, one warm-world parent) have variable coats — most commonly *salt-and-pepper* (white guard-hairs over a gold-russet undercoat) or *piebald* (irregular patches of white and gold). The empathic-substrate inheritance is uncorrelated with coat pattern; a salt-and-pepper Furling might have full warm-world empathy or none. **Mixed-coats are subject to a quiet cultural suspicion in both directions** — warm-world Furlings wonder if they will betray; cold-world Furlings wonder if they will weaken. Most mixed-coats find their own way. (The Steward may or may not be one; this is at Design's discretion if a coat trait is added to the character.)

**Cleanser uniform / aesthetic**: white fur is already prominent. Cleansers tend toward stark dark-violet or deep-blue formal wear (the warm-world Furlings wear warm rust and ember-gold; the contrast is deliberate). The Cleanser Cruiser's hull palette canonized in [cleanser-encounter-design.md §"Visual"](cleanser-encounter-design.md) — interior `(40, 50, 80)`, rim `(200, 180, 240)` — is congruent: cold-violet on a Furling-base hull, the colors the Cleanser crew wear, the colors the species' ancestral ice-mirages produced in their oldest songs.

## 4. Aggressive stubbornness — behavioral canon

In combat, in negotiation, in personal commitment: once a Cleanser has decided, they execute. The decision-revision cost is so high that they will continue down a path even when better options become visible mid-task, because the cognitive cost of acknowledging the new option exceeds the marginal benefit. They are not stupid — they are *committed*. The bias is anatomical, not chosen.

This affects multiple slice systems:

- **Combat AI**: the existing canonical Cleanser combat profile is `ai_style = "kiter"` with high-caution / high-hesitation. **The high-hesitation is now retracted.** Cleansers are NOT hesitant. They are high-caution (preserve the ship, complete the mission) but they fire when the math says to fire and they pursue when the math says to pursue. The "hesitation" appearance in early designs was a confusion between *cultural mourning* (the gentle sorrow performed during Vael-Souren's encounter dialog) and *biological hesitation* (which does not exist in this lineage). Combat AI should weight: high accuracy, high commitment, low retreat tendency. They *will* fight to the death if the doctrine commits them.

- **Dialog**: a Cleanser who has accepted a delay (negotiated path) **will honor it precisely** — to the second, not to the spirit. They do not soften under appeal once committed; they also do not betray their own word. Vael-Souren's "Two cycles. I will not be lied to, Steward" is canonical: the commitment runs both ways. The negotiation-success branch can be trusted; the negotiation-failure branch executes with the same precision.

- **Personal**: Cleansers who form attachments (to crewmates, to Stewards, to a particular cluster's people) commit to those attachments with the same stubbornness. The cold-mirror substrate does not prevent loyalty; it just makes loyalty a *decision* rather than a *feeling*. A Cleanser friend is the most reliable kind of friend; the friendship arrived through choice, and a Cleanser's choices are durable.

## 5. No empathy — the heart of the canon

This is the trait that costs the slice's central morality the most to grapple with. The Cleansers are *not monsters* — they have a robust ethical framework, they observe duty, they keep their word, they record every euthanized species in the Quiet Ledger and they mourn each entry. **The mourning is real to them as a discipline. It is not, biologically, a felt grief.**

Three implications:

**5.1 The mourning is performed, not felt.**
The Quiet Ledger ritual is the Cleansers' cultural answer to the absence of natural feeling. They have constructed an elaborate, sincere, multi-generational practice of memorial work — names spoken, archives maintained, recorded silences observed on the dates of each Cleansing. They do this because their *cognition* recognizes that the deaths matter and the doctrine that demands the deaths must also demand witness. They do not do this because they feel the deaths.

Vael-Souren's line *"I will mourn longer than they will be missed by anyone else in this universe. That is the price I pay. It is the only price I am asked to pay"* — canonical from cleanser-encounter-design.md — must now be read with this update. Vael-Souren is not saying they will grieve harder than anyone. They are saying they will *execute the discipline of mourning* longer than anyone, because the discipline is their species' atonement-mechanism. The grief itself is shaped by the discipline, not by spontaneous feeling. It is no less real for being chosen.

**5.2 Some Cleansers crack.**
Not all ice-branch Furlings carry the empathic-substrate reduction equally. The trait varies along a normal distribution like any heritable feature. A small minority of Cleansers — perhaps one in twenty — carry enough warm-world empathic substrate (typically from a mixed-coat ancestor several generations back) to *feel* the Cleansings as warm-world Furlings would. These Cleansers either:
- Defect to the Persuader faction (and are quietly accepted),
- Stay in the Cleanser faction and break under the load (the slice's most tragic Cleanser variant — a Cleanser who can no longer perform the discipline because the substrate is supplying actual grief at a volume the doctrine was not designed to absorb), or
- Adopt extreme stoic practices to suppress the feeling (the rare *Cold-Mask* Cleansers, who out-perform the standard discipline by sheer force of will).

This variation is **not currently a slice mechanic** but it is **available for any Cleanser-NPC** the slice surfaces. Vael-Souren herself is canonically baseline (no warm-world admixture); her gentleness is discipline, not warmth.

**5.3 The "no empathy" frame is not an excuse.**
The Cleansers do not invoke their biology to absolve their actions. They take responsibility for what they do. The substrate is the tool; the choice is theirs. They could (and the Persuaders argue they should) refuse the Cleansing work and let the warm-world Furlings figure out another way. The Cleansers' position is that there is no other way, that the warm-world Furlings would not be able to do the work even if they accepted the math, and that volunteering their biology to a job no one else can do is the most coherent form of service their lineage can offer.

The Persuaders find this argument intolerable. The Defenders find it incomprehensible. The Compellers — the pragmatists — find it convenient. The Cleansers find it true.

## 6. The Vote — total cleansing of the stay-behinds

> **Canon update (2026-05-17, Aaron dispatch):** *"Their vote is to wipe out the life that stays behind, even the friends like the grounded species just to be safe."*

The Cleansers' position in any Council deliberation about a non-migrating species is, with very few exceptions, **euthanize**. This is not limited to the difficult cases — the Homesteader-Deniers, the unrepentant Defenders, the species who actively refuse the Migration. The Cleansers vote to euthanize the friends as well: the species who *cannot* migrate (Slylandro tied to their gas giant, Chenjesu rooted in stone, Mycon biots locked to mantle, Mmrnmhrm bound to their iron-rich crust, Taalo ecosystem-dependent), the species who could be cloaked but the cloak is theoretical, the species who could be hidden but the hiding is unverified, the species below the Others' threshold whose cognition might evolve someday, the proto-species who are pre-sentient now but may become sentient post-Migration when the Furlings are no longer here to guide them. **Every species that does not depart this galaxy is, in the Cleanser vote, a name written into the Quiet Ledger.**

### 6.1 The doctrinal logic — asymmetric risk weighting

- A **missed euthanization risks the cycle**. If the Others detect a single surviving species post-Migration, the destination galaxy may be compromised; the cycle may not break; the next visitation may find Furling descendants among the prey. The Chenjesu testimony (per [the-mmrnmhrm-and-chenjesu.md](the-mmrnmhrm-and-chenjesu.md)) canonizes the cycle as real. The Cleanser calculus puts a heavy weight on that probability, however small.

- An **unnecessary euthanization costs a species**. The cost is finite and bounded.

- Therefore: **the Cleansers vote to euthanize every grounded species** the Council surfaces, regardless of friendship, alliance, or below-threshold status. *"Just to be safe"* is the doctrinal phrase. The phrase is sincere. They have argued it for generations.

### 6.2 "Even the friends"

This is the part warm-world Furlings cannot stomach. The Slylandro have been the Furlings' eldest non-Furling sentience for ten thousand years. The Chenjesu have shared the cycle-testimony that may save the species. The Taalo are makers and gentle pacifists. The Cleansers vote to euthanize all of them — not because the Cleansers dislike them, but because the substrate that would make their friendship *matter to the vote* is not present. The Cleansers know these species are friends. **They vote anyway.**

This is the doctrinal application of the no-empathy substrate at the political level. A warm-world Furling, asked to vote to euthanize a friend whose biology binds them to a world we are leaving, *cannot bring themselves to do it* — the affective-mirroring substrate floods the decision with felt-grief that outweighs the risk calculus, and the vote becomes *no*. A Cleanser, asked the same question, does the math. The math says *yes*. The vote follows the math.

The warm-world Furlings have at times asked Cleansers *whether* they understand that the species in question are friends. The Cleansers respond, in essence: *yes, we are aware. The friendship is in the record. The Quiet Ledger will name them with full honor. The vote remains.* This response is the source of the deepest tension within the Furling Council — Persuaders find it not just wrong but *uncanny*, a thinking that does not break the way a person's thinking should break.

### 6.3 Who the vote covers — and who it doesn't

**Cleansers vote to euthanize**:
- All Homesteader-faction species (definition: staying behind), whether by refusal or by biology
- All "grounded" species who cannot migrate even if they wanted to (Slylandro, Chenjesu, Mycon, Mmrnmhrm, Taalo, and any analog)
- All species whose preservation path is *theoretical* — cloak (Slylandro Satellite), hide (Hider research), devolve (Utwig Veils), shield (Taalo Protector), sub-threshold (Chenjesu lattice). All of these are unverified at slice-era; the Cleansers consider unverified equivalent to insufficient.
- All proto-species who could cross the sentience threshold post-Migration — proto-Spathi, proto-Yehat, proto-Pkunk, proto-VUX, proto-Druuge, proto-Ilwrath, proto-Shofixti, proto-Thraddash, proto-Zoq-Fot-Pik, proto-Syreen, proto-Supox, proto-Utwig, the proto-Humans on Sol III, and the unfinished proto-Ur-Quan / proto-Qor-Ah uplift. Same logic: any of them could become detectable to the next cycle without Furling guidance present to manage it.

**Cleansers do NOT vote to euthanize**:
- Migrating species (Burvixese, Karavem, Kovellim, Selvenne, Stelloth, Melnorme, Androsynth, Arilou, all Precursor-faction species crossing the threshold). No doctrinal need; the species are leaving; the Quiet will be sufficient.
- Furlings themselves, including Defender-faction Furlings who refuse to migrate. The Cleansers would entertain the calculation; the Council never sanctions it. Furling-on-Furling euthanasia is one of the few lines even the Cleansers respect — the lineage stops at the lineage's edge.
- The Arilou Voluntary-Exile contingent. Quasi-Space exile counts as departure under doctrinal accounting; the Arilou are "gone" in the sense the doctrine cares about.

### 6.4 Slice implications

- **The Slylandro Cloaking Satellite path is canonically a Persuader victory *against* the standing Cleanser vote.** The Council deliberates; the Persuaders argue the cloak is sufficient; the Cleansers argue it is theoretical and the species should be euthanized to be safe; the Steward's recommendation (and the Slylandro Outreach work the Steward has done) tips the balance. **Vael-Souren's arrival in Act 3 is the Cleanser faction acting on its standing vote** — she is dispatched because the deliberation has either resolved in the Cleansers' favor *or* the Cleansers have decided to act before resolution. The cloak's victory at the dialog table is a *vote-override*, not a doctrinal consensus.
- **Every grounded species in the slice has a parallel Cleanser-vote attached.** Mycon (whether or not the Deep Child whispers awaken), Mmrnmhrm (continuously improving), Chenjesu (might evolve), Taalo (Shield is theoretical), Slylandro (cloak is theoretical), Utwig (devolution might reverse), Lemmkin (chose to stay), Mrokon (defiant). Each is a Cleanser-voted euthanization that the Persuader path is trying to prevent. The slice's stay-behind saves are each one a vote the Steward has helped tip the other way.
- **Proto-species are in the vote too.** Including the proto-Humans on Sol III. The Persuader vote wins for the proto-species in baseline canon — *most species are better off left alone* is the canonical thematic counterweight (per [proto-species-observations.md](proto-species-observations.md)) — but the Cleanser vote was *cast against*, and a Steward whose recommendations push the Council Cleanser-ward could change that. **A Cleanser victory on the proto-species vote is canonically how the SC2-era species would be erased pre-history** — every proto-ancestor euthanized "just to be safe." (This is a doctrinal extreme; not slice-MVP-achievable on the strict math; flagged as a future ending-tier consideration if Aaron wants it. Provisional name: **"The Sterile Galaxy" ending** — a deeper failure mode than Unsuccessful, where the Migration succeeds *because the galaxy was emptied of life that could have failed it*. The Quiet Ledger in that ending is the only record left.)
- **The Council vote is the actual moral machinery of the slice.** The Steward's species-by-species recommendations are individual votes in a multi-species deliberation; each one ratifies or rejects the standing Cleanser position for that species. The Persuader / Compeller / Cleanser standing tracking already in the slice's flag system is the visible-record of this ongoing vote.
- **The next Cleanser arrives with the full backlog.** If the Steward defeats Vael-Souren in Stand-Against combat, the Cleanser faction does not disengage from the cluster. Vael-Souren is not unique; she is a Cleanser doing standard Cleanser work. **Killing one Cleanser does not retract the vote.** A second Cleanser arrives at the next standing-vote threshold; if the Steward intercepts and kills them too, a third. The Cleansers are not stupid — they will eventually escalate (cruiser-pair, then fleet) — but the vote itself does not bend. (Design: not slice-MVP; flagged as a deepening hook for any extended-slice or full-game content. The slice resolves with Vael-Souren as the only Cleanser encounter the Steward faces.)

### 6.5 The phrase

When a Cleanser is asked, in any register — formal Council, private conversation, end-of-combat surrender, deathbed — *why even the friends*, the canonical response is some variant of: **"Just to be safe."** The phrase is doctrinal; every Cleanser knows it; every warm-world Furling has heard it; the Persuaders consider it the three most chilling words in the Furling language.

Vael-Souren has the phrase available in any dialog branch where the Steward asks her *why the Slylandro, why now, why a species we have lived with for ten thousand years*. Design's call whether to surface it as a player-elected dialog choice or to keep it ambient — both are canonical. The phrase should land cold.

## 7. Encounter implications — what changes for the slice

This canon update **does not invalidate** the existing Cleanser-encounter design. Vael-Souren's dialog still reads the same. The combat still happens the same way. The branching still resolves the same way. The update **adds a layer underneath** the existing presentation:

- **Vael-Souren's white fur** is now canonical. Portraits and renderings should reflect this. The dense double-coat, pale eyes, broader shoulders, slightly stockier stature relative to other Furling NPCs. Her formal wear is cold-violet, in canonical Cleanser-uniform palette.
- **Her gentle-sorrowful register** is now framed as performed discipline. The voice direction is unchanged on the surface (gentle, measured, mournful) but a perceptive listener can hear the *precision* — the modulation is at the upper end of her natural vocal range, suggesting effort, not ease. This is a subtle layer for Audio to consider; players will not consciously notice and that is the point.
- **The combat banter** (per the parallel chat's banter-library.md: Cleanser canonically grieving across damage brackets) is **still canonically grieving** — but it is *disciplined* grief, not *felt* grief. The lines do not change. The acting direction does. The Cleanser-Cruiser AI's "hesitation" parameter is retracted; she fires when the math says.
- **The Cleanser-supervised Halia branch** (the worst Fall variant from the parallel chat's the-final-conflict.md) is **visually devastated** by this canon: the supervisors flanking Halia are visibly white-furred. The image reads as *the cold ones have arrived*. Halia's warm-gold fur against the supervisors' bright white. Her *"I'm sorry, Steward"* whisper is across that visual gap.
- **The Steward's reaction**: the Steward is canonically a warm-world Furling (the protagonist's empathy is the slice's emotional center; the humor doctrine assumes warm-world affective response). Their first sight of a white-furred Furling at close range should land as a small recognition — *oh, the other kind* — followed by everything else they bring to the encounter. This is a layer Design might surface as a one-line internal recognition; not required.

## 9. Open questions (Aaron-call if any of these matter)

- **Are the Steward's parents both warm-world Furlings, both ice-branch, or one of each?** The default canon is *both warm-world*; the Steward's empathic responses are baseline. If the slice wants to surface a *mixed-coat Steward* variant (rare; emotional resonance varied), that could be a future-pass character option. Not slice-MVP.
- **Is Halia warm-world or mixed?** Default canon: warm-world. Her warmth is felt, not performed. The Cleanser-supervised Fall branch's tragedy depends on this — the supervisors do not feel what Halia is feeling. If Halia were mixed, the branch reads differently.
- **Does the ice-branch maintain a separate political tradition within the Furling Council?** Default canon: the Cleansers ARE the ice-branch's political tradition — the faction and the lineage coincide so completely that distinguishing them is mostly an academic exercise. If a non-Cleanser ice-branch Furling exists (warm-world doctrine but cold-world biology), they would be unusual but not unheard-of; Aaron may want to author one for a side-quest someday. Not slice-MVP.
- **Did the Furlings ever try to engineer the empathic-substrate reduction into warm-world volunteers** (the *Voluntary-Cold* program — accept the biological intervention, do the Cleanser work, sleep through the Migration)? Default canon: **no**. The Council considered it twice in deep history and rejected both proposals on the grounds that *manufacturing the absence of empathy is not the same as inheriting it*, and the resulting Furlings would be neither truly warm-world nor truly ice-branch. The Cleansers themselves opposed the program — they considered it a counterfeit of their identity. If Aaron wants to surface this as a fringe Compeller-faction position, it is available.
