# Factions and the Furling War

> Companion to [the-furlings-and-the-others.md](the-furlings-and-the-others.md) and [the-precursors-and-homesteaders.md](the-precursors-and-homesteaders.md). The Precursors-and-Homesteaders doc defines the high-level **Stay (Homesteaders) vs. Go (Precursors)** binary; this doc enumerates the *method-factions* within each side and explains how the war among them shapes the player's slice.

The Migration plan has three viable strategies — Migrate, Cloak, Hide-in-Quasi-Space — and one unviable one (Fight). Settling the political question of *which species takes which strategy* turns into civil war. The war isn't fundamentally about whether the Others are coming; both sides accept that (the Deniers excepted). The war is over **what to do with species who refuse to migrate AND refuse to cloak AND refuse to hide**.

## The Methods Toolkit

The player's verbs against any species are: **Convince, Intimidate, Dominate, Eliminate**, plus the alternatives **Cloak** and **Hide** (the Arilou path). Different method-factions prefer different verbs. Full design in [win-condition-and-methods.md](win-condition-and-methods.md). Quick mapping:

- **Persuaders** → Convince
- **Compellers (mild)** → Intimidate
- **Compellers (hard)** → Dominate
- **Cleansers** → Eliminate
- **Hiders** → Cloak (build for clients) or Hide (with Arilou)

## The High-Level Split — Where Each Method-Faction Sits

The method-factions enumerated below all sit under either the **Precursors** (Go) or **Homesteaders** (Stay) umbrella. Quick alignment table:

| Method-Faction | Side (Stay/Go) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Persuaders | **Precursor** | Lead the Migration; do the diplomatic work |
| Compellers | **Precursor** | Pragmatists; sedation/deception for stubborn Homesteader cases |
| Cleansers | **Precursor** (extreme) | Euthanize uncloakable Homesteaders to keep the Quiet sufficient |
| Defenders | **Homesteader** | Refuse to flee; build the Sa-Matra; stay and fight |
| Deniers | **Homesteader** | Reject the threat entirely |
| Hiders | **Split**: cloak-and-leave (Precursor) / cloak-and-stay (Homesteader) / join the Arilou (third path) |

The Hider faction is the most interesting because their *engineering* (the Slylandro Cloaking Satellite, dimensional folds, sub-threshold-emission research) is used by all three paths. They are the technical wing of the entire debate; their politics depend on which client they're building for.

The faction details below remain as-is, with the understanding that they sit under the Stay/Go umbrella.

## The Furling Factions

The factions are not formally chartered. They are tendencies, reading-rooms, mutual-aid circles that ossify into something more dangerous over the decades of debate. By the slice's era, they have organized fleets, agents, and doctrines. They communicate in code. They sometimes shoot at each other.

### Persuaders — *Diplomats of the Migration*
**Doctrine:** Every species will eventually agree to leave. Patience, careful translation, and respect for self-determination will get the work done. Coercion creates resistance that only grows the Defenders and the Holdouts. Take the time the schedule allows.
**Leverage:** Most Slylandro contact, most Arilou cooperation, most species-of-good-will. The moral high ground. **The canonical Commander of the Furling Defense Fleet — Halia — is a Persuader-faction Furling who keeps the Fleet on a Persuader mission (escort evacuating species; secure migration corridors; last-resort grounded-species defense; search-and-rescue).** The Defense Fleet is the broader Furling military formation, *not* faction-aligned — Halia's command is canonically why the Fleet serves the Migration rather than the Defenders' stay-and-fight doctrine. The Defenders canonically resent this; Drev-Tok has filed standing motions to redirect Fleet assets for two years. Halia has declined every one. See [halia-profile.md](halia-profile.md).
**Weakness:** Slow. The schedule is not flexible. Every season they spend persuading a stubborn species is a season closer to the Others.
**In the slice:** The Council's initial briefing voice is a Persuader — Halia, who delivers the canonical Quiet Resolution mandate to the Steward. The Persuader path is the canonical "good" ending — but it requires the player to make tight time management calls.

### Compellers — *Engineers of Necessity*
**Doctrine:** The end justifies *non-cruel* means. Persuasion is preferred but cannot always finish in time. Sedation, controlled deception, *firm* extraction — these are the costs of getting people out alive. *"Better to wake up confused and free than to die from principle."*
**Leverage:** Logistics, ship-loading, fleet operations. The Furlings who actually move biomass through portals.
**Weakness:** Loathed by both Persuaders (who think they're crossing a line) and Cleansers (who think they're naive). They get the work done and no one thanks them.
**In the slice:** Probably the path the player takes when "the schedule" pushes them. Lies about Arilou destinations, "evacuation drills" that are actual departures, etc.

### Cleansers — *The Quiet Ledger*
**Doctrine:** The Quiet must be absolute. A single hold-out species defeats the entire Migration; the Others will linger, find every hidden enclave, and — by the Chenjesu testimony — return in cycles to find Furling descendants among the prey. The Cleansers' vote, therefore, **is to wipe out the life that stays behind — even the friends like the grounded species — just to be safe.** The doctrine extends to every grounded species (Slylandro, Chenjesu, Mycon, Mmrnmhrm, Taalo, Lemmkin, Mrokon, Utwig), every species whose preservation path is *theoretical* (cloaks, shields, devolution doctrines, sub-threshold cognition — all unverified at slice-era), and every proto-species who could cross the sentience threshold post-Migration without Furling guidance present. Friendship is in the record; the Quiet Ledger names every species with full honor; the vote remains. Each death is recorded and ritually mourned as a *performed cultural discipline* (their substrate does not generate the grief spontaneously — see [cleansers-as-ice-branch.md](cleansers-as-ice-branch.md) §5-6). They consider themselves the deepest mercy in the Migration. The canonical doctrinal phrase, when asked *why even the friends*: **"Just to be safe."**
**Lineage:** The Cleansers are biologically the **ice-branch of the Furling species** — white-furred, cold-world-descended, anatomically reduced in empathic-mirroring substrate. They are not a separate species; they interbreed with the warm-world Furling majority and share the same Council. But their cold-world ancestry produced three observable differences: white fur, aggressive stubbornness (high decision-revision cost), and a substrate that does not natively *feel* the suffering of others. **The faction doctrine and the lineage emerged together; one shaped the other.** The Cleansers' ritual mourning of every name in the Quiet Ledger is a *performed cultural discipline*, sincere and durable, layered over a biology that does not generate the grief spontaneously. Full canon: [cleansers-as-ice-branch.md](cleansers-as-ice-branch.md).
**Leverage:** Quiet weapons. Disease-engineering biots. Stellar-radiation manipulation. The Sa-Matra was originally intended as a Defender weapon, but Cleansers have studied its applications against single planets.
**Weakness:** Morally radioactive. Persuaders and most Compellers will not work with them openly. Most species who learn of Cleanser methods turn against the Migration entirely.
**In the slice:** A Cleanser emissary arrives in Act 3 demanding the Slylandro evacuation be ended (one way or another). They are not a cartoon villain. Their voice is gentle, sorrowful, certain. They have done this before. They will do it again.

### Defenders — *We Will Not Run*
**Doctrine:** Fleeing is surrender. The Furlings are the greatest civilization the galaxy has ever known, and the answer to a predator is to *fight*. Build the Sa-Matra. Build the planet-shielding biots. Hold the line. Maybe the Others can be hurt; maybe they can be deflected; maybe at minimum the Furlings can buy the rest of the galaxy time. *Anything is better than abandoning our home.*
**Leverage:** Military hardware, the Sa-Matra construction program, alliances with species whose homeworlds they vow to defend (proto-Yehat warriors, proto-Druuge industrialists).
**Weakness:** The math is bad. Every Furling who plans to defend is one fewer through the portal. Every successful weapon makes the Others more interested. The Defenders' efforts may actively *attract* the Culling.
**In the slice:** Distant rumors. A Defender courier passes through the cluster in Act 3 begging the player to abandon evacuation and bring resources home for the war. Optional.

### Deniers — *The Others Are A Story*
**Doctrine:** The dimensional ripples are real but explainable. The Furling Council is panicking. The "Others" are a metaphysical fiction generated by anxiety. The Migration is a power grab — an attempt by the elite to consolidate the species in a single new galaxy where they will dominate. *Stay. Live your life. Trust the galaxy that has always nurtured you.*
**Leverage:** Civilian sympathy. Many species find this faction more comforting than the truth.
**Weakness:** Wrong. The Others are real. The Deniers' propaganda creates Holdouts who then become targets of the Cleansers, which is the cruelest irony in the entire war.
**In the slice:** Subtle. A Slylandro elder repeats a Denier talking-point in Act 2. The player can correct, agree, or stay silent.

### Preservers — *The Slow Branch* (Migration-Positive Pacifist)
**Doctrine:** Every being can be loved into the better choice. The Migration is the better choice — leaving is choosing-life, staying is grieving-in-place. The Preservers do not push, do not extract, do not deceive. They embody the choice by being present, gentle, and unhurried; species who spend time with Preservers come to want what Preservers want. They actively encourage species who *can* migrate to do so — visiting Homesteader communities, sharing fruit and song, demonstrating Migration as a positive-affect choice rather than a survival-flight. **They refuse to hurt anything**, and they consider every species' choice (even the Homesteader choice to stay) sacred — they do not vote to euthanize anyone, ever, including Furlings refusing to migrate. The Preserver vote is the canonical *opposite* of the Cleanser vote: **preserve every species' choice**, even when the choice may be wrong.
**Lineage:** The Preservers are biologically the **tree-branch of the Furling species** — green-furred, forest-canopy-descended, **photosynthetically self-sustaining**, with an enhanced empathic-mirroring substrate that produces *amplified felt-harm* when they contemplate hurting another being. Their pacifism is not chosen; it is what the metabolism allows. They live in trees, sing in choirs, and move slowly. **The faction doctrine and the lineage emerged together** — the inverse of the Cleansers' ice-branch biology and the antithesis of the Cleanser vote. Full canon: [preservers-as-tree-branch.md](preservers-as-tree-branch.md).
**Leverage:** Soft-power diplomacy at species the Persuaders could not reach. The Slylandro Cloaking Satellite path is partly the result of Preserver ambassador work over decades. Strong alliance with Persuaders, cooperative with Hiders, counter-evangelism against Deniers, absolute opposition to Cleansers (Preserver ships have on record physically positioned themselves between Cleanser cruisers and target planets as moral witness). **Their Canopy-class vessels carry the only non-lethal-by-design combat kit in the slice's roster** — Gravity Well Projection + Gravitational Jump; immobilize, reposition, never directly damage. Canonical phrase when collisions result: ***"We did not hurt them. They were caught in a gradient. They could have moved differently."***
**Weakness:** Slow. The slowest of any pro-Migration faction; the canopy-cognition substrate cannot be hurried. Species who need urgent extraction (deadline-pressed Homesteaders) cannot wait for the Preserver method. Their non-lethal combat kit is *vulnerable to attrition* — a determined attacker can wear them down; if they cannot flee, they die. They lose more ships per engagement than any other Furling faction.
**In the slice:** **Lirin Pel-Sa** is the canonical Preserver ambassador at Beta Corvi, having lived with the Slylandro for forty years; their groundwork is part of why the Cloaking Satellite path is viable. A Canopy-class vessel (*Long-Reach Of The Slow Branch*, captain **Veth Pel-Sa** — Lirin's elder sibling) appears in cluster hyperspace; the Steward can dock and visit, decline politely, or attack (heavy reputation consequences if killed). A Preserver canopy-village on a tree-rich cluster world hosts a Taalo guest (sibling-substrate-fragment of Vresh's colony — see [crew-roster-redesign.md](crew-roster-redesign.md)) and offers the Steward fruit-and-song, a living-plant cutting, and a brief Preserver chorus. **The Preserver Council voice argues the *preserve-the-choice* doctrine on every grounded-species recommendation**, opposite the Cleanser *"Just to be safe"* — canonical counter-line: *"We will not be safer for having killed them. The Quiet you propose is the Quiet of a closed grave. Our Quiet must be the Quiet of a continuing song, even if some voices in it are not ours."*

### Hiders — *Pockets of Quiet*
**Doctrine:** Maybe a galaxy doesn't need to be fully evacuated. Maybe a small enclave — a single shielded planet, a star system wrapped in dimensional baffling — can hide from the Others' senses. *Why move everyone if we can hide some?*
**Leverage:** Some shared research with the Arilou (who hid in Quasi-Space, after all). Limited but interesting tech.
**Weakness:** Untested at scale. The Others' senses are not well-mapped. One leak ends the Migration's effectiveness.
**In the slice:** The Mycon biots, if they awaken into Deep Child sentience, might be candidates for the Hider strategy — they live underground, surrounded by spore-clouds that might mask mind-light. This is a genuinely-debated option in the player's Council reports.

### Talos — *Be Loud. Be Heard. Be Spared.* (Homesteader, Stay-Side)
**Doctrine:** The Others sense cognition. Fine — *meet them with cognition*. Build a transmitter that broadcasts our intelligence at such an overwhelming amplitude that the Others must either parley, recognize a peer, or simply pass us over as too costly to engage. *Hiding is cowardice; running is surrender; defending is suicide. The fourth path is to be brilliantly, deafeningly unignorable.*
**Leverage:** The **Talos Resonator** — a Furling-built signal-amplifier device tuned to project the operator's full cognitive bandwidth across dimensional substrates. Mid-stage prototype during the slice; nearly operational by mid-game.
**Weakness:** **Catastrophically wrong.** The Others do not parley. They do not recognize peers. They do not pass over the loud. They *eat the loud first*. The Talos broadcast is the equivalent of a dinner bell.
**In the slice (mid-game event):** A small Talos faction enclave operating in a peripheral cluster system completes its Resonator and activates it. The player witnesses the broadcast — a brief moment of *enormous* signal flooding every comm channel, a defiant message in Furling — then **silence**, followed by an Other arrival that erases the system within minutes. The Talos device itself survives (it was built into the planet's mantle); the Others ignore the inert hardware after the broadcasters are gone.
**SC2 callforward:** the surviving **Talos Resonator** is recoverable as a Furling-era artifact. SC2-era humans find one of these devices intact — its operators long dead — and recognize it as Precursor technology. It becomes a famous SC2 artifact: a still-functional broadcaster of unknown purpose. SC2 captains treat it as a mystery. The slice player knows what it really is: a martyr's loud goodbye.

## The Species' Responses (slice-relevant only)

### Slylandro (your slice's main sentient species)
**Initial response:** awe, grief, curiosity. They want to understand the Others before deciding.
**Faction lean:** mostly Persuader-friendly. A vocal minority lean Denier ("we have lived in our gas giants for 100,000 years; the Furlings cannot really know an interdimensional predator exists").
**Decision speed:** slow. They process every implication. They debate for *centuries* in their gas-current consciousness. They do not have centuries.
**Player's task:** convince them, lie to them, or step aside.

### Mycon biots (your slice's terraformers)
**Initial response:** they are non-sentient. They cannot respond.
**Twist:** the Deep Child whispers are *early sentience*. The biots are becoming people. If the player ignores the whispers, the awakening accelerates. If the player suppresses the whispers, the biots might never become sentient (and stay safely outside the evacuation requirement). If the player *accepts* and shepherds the awakening, the Furlings have one more species to evacuate.
**Faction implication:** the Cleansers want the biots burned before sentience completes. The Persuaders want to wait and see. The Hiders see the biots as test subjects for the Hider strategy. The proto-Mycon are *innocent* in any framing, which is what makes their fate so hard.

### Proto-Ur-Quan limpets
**Initial response:** none. They are non-sentient.
**The cruel question:** the Council had been debating uplifting them for centuries. The Migration changes the math: an uplift *now* creates a species that will need evacuation in the same decade the Others arrive. The honest answer is "do not uplift them." The proto-Ur-Quan will live, undisturbed, through the Culling because they will be invisible to the Others. They will eventually become sentient on their own — millions of years from now, in a galaxy that will have re-grown.
**Slice's quiet horror:** to save them, *leave them as animals*. The decision to *not* uplift is the kindest decision the Furlings will ever make, and they will hate it.

### Arilou
**Initial response:** they crossed early. They come back through the portal occasionally with help and warnings.
**Faction:** loosely Persuader-aligned but refuse to take direct sides. They have seen what awaits in the new galaxy and do not want to argue with anyone who hasn't.
**Player's task:** accept their help, share data, but maybe not depend on them.

### Background races (off-screen)
- **Mael-Num** (proto-Sentient Milieu) — divided. Some embrace the Migration. Others see it as Furling expansionism.
- **Chenjesu** (proto-Chmmr) — crystalline ancients. They prefer the Hider strategy; their crystal lattices are theoretically opaque to Other senses. Untested.
- **Other proto-species** (Yehat, Pkunk, VUX, etc.) — all pre-sentient. The Migration debate doesn't apply to them yet, but the Cleansers are watching to make sure no rogue Persuader uplifts them.

## The War's Phases (full game; slice covers the early-middle)

1. **Discovery Era** (decades before the slice): the Furlings learn of the Others. Internal panic. Migration plan adopted by Council majority.
2. **Diplomatic Era** (about a generation): peaceful evacuation of cooperating species. Persuaders dominate. Most of the easy species have left.
3. **Coercion Era** (the slice's present): the hold-outs are the stubborn cases. Compellers ascendant. Cleansers organizing in shadow. **This is where the player operates.**
4. **Civil War Era** (just beginning at the slice's end): Furling factions openly fighting. Defender militias raid evacuation fleets. Cleansers commit visible atrocities. The Sa-Matra goes from "joint defense weapon" to "contested asset."
5. **The Closing of the Door** (after the slice): the dimensional crossing is closed by the Arilou-Persuader alliance to prevent further conflict from leaking into the new galaxy. Whoever is on this side stays on this side.
6. **The Culling** (after the door closes): the Others arrive. The galaxy goes silent. Most holdouts die. Some Hider enclaves survive (notably Chenjesu, awakening Mycon). The Sa-Matra hurts the Others enough to make them move on faster — possibly the only reason the Arilou's return-path stays open.
7. **The Long Quiet** (millennia): the galaxy bacterial again. The proto-Ur-Quan limpets crawl on tide-pools.
8. **The Return** (SC2's prologue, much later): species from the neighboring galaxy filter back. Some find the Sa-Matra. Some find the awakened Mycon. Some find the Slylandro who survived in their gas-clouds because the Cloaking Satellite worked. The SC2 era begins.

## Implications for the Player

- Combat in the slice is mostly **between Furling factions** or **between Furlings and resisting species**. The "Them-rift creature" we sketched earlier is rare; the more interesting fight is the Cleanser cruiser the player must engage if they choose to protect the Slylandro.
- Every dialog encounter has a **faction-pressure** dimension. The Slylandro know the Cleansers exist (rumors). The Mycon, if sentient, fear extermination. The Arilou have opinions about every Furling action.
- The player's **disposition with each species and each faction** is tracked. Side with the Cleansers once and the Persuader voice in the Council briefings turns cold. Save the Slylandro and the Cleanser doctrine shifts from "tactical opposition" to "personal enmity."
- The slice's *combat boss* is therefore probably a **Cleanser ship**, not a monster. This recontextualizes the combat from "shoot the Other-thing" to "the hardest fight is against your own kind."

## The Moral Heart

This game is, ultimately, about being asked to save the people you love by helping kill some of them — and choosing whether you can. There is no clean answer. There is no path where the Furlings emerge unstained.

The SC2 universe is what's left of that choice, weathered by 250,000 years. Players who know SC2 will notice that the Ur-Quan slavery, the Mycon planet-burning, the Kohr-Ah genocide — all the cruelties of the SC2 universe — are descendants of choices made by people like the player. The Furlings did not just leave gifts behind. They left **modes of cruelty** that the survivors continued to practice without knowing why.

That's the game.
