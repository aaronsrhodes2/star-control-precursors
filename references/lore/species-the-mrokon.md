# The Mrokon — *The Defiant* (puppet-warrior operators; the Hammer-Of-Refusal)

> Authored 2026-05-17 (Skippy creative pass). Replaces the THE_DEFIANT /
> THE_DEFIANT_BACKLOG placeholder rows in the species spreadsheet. Aaron's
> brief: "go exotic." Exotic vector: **each Mrokon "warrior" the Steward
> meets is not a person but a deep-linked combat-puppet remote-controlled
> by a small Operator hidden in an underground bunker. Operators routinely
> burn through 4-6 puppets per lifetime. The Hammer-Of-Refusal is their
> last collective gesture — when the Culling arrives, every Operator
> simultaneously fires their last puppet's kinetic-impact load at the
> Others. *They die marking the predator.***

## The Concept

The Mrokon are a species in two parts:

1. **Operators** — the actual sentient beings. Small (~1m tall, mostly
   head, child-sized). Live in deep-shielded underground bunkers
   designed for centuries of operational use. Operators rarely emerge
   to the surface; they live, work, and die underground. A single
   Operator lifespan is ~120 years.
2. **Puppets** — heavy armored bipedal combat-bodies (~2.3m, fully
   weaponized, redundantly-armored). Each Operator remote-controls
   one puppet at a time via deep-link neural interface. Puppets are
   *expendable*. When a puppet dies in combat or to environment, the
   Operator switches to a fresh puppet from the bunker's puppet-vault.
   A typical Operator burns through 4-6 puppets in a lifetime.

To a visitor on Mrokon's surface, the species *appears* as a planet
of heavy-armored warriors. To the visitor who *kills* a Mrokon
warrior, the body falls — but the Operator simply takes a new
puppet and resumes within minutes. **The Steward cannot win an
attrition fight on Mrokon.**

The Operators have built this system over ~3,000 years of
species-evolution: their cognitive flares were once large enough
to register with prior-cycle Others. **The puppet-doctrine is a
biological adaptation to remote-presence — by *not being on the
surface*, the Operator's cognitive signature is masked by ~2km of
shielded rock.** This nearly drops them below threshold by itself.

Then there is the second part of the doctrine — the part that
makes them *Defiant*. The Operators learned that even from
underground, *combat-action by their puppets* spikes their
cognitive signature briefly. They built up to this: they want to
*use* the spike. They want the Others to *see them coming*.

## The Hammer-Of-Refusal (their canonical last gesture)

When the Culling arrives, every Operator on Mrokon simultaneously:

1. Synchronizes with their last available puppet
2. The puppet fires its **maximum-yield kinetic-impact round** —
   a tungsten-uranium slug propelled by Mrokon's largest cannon-array,
   tuned to dimensional-resonance frequencies that the Mrokon
   discovered (with Furling engineering help) *can briefly disrupt
   the Others' substrate*
3. The puppet immediately destroys itself in the recoil shockwave
4. The Operator dies in the deep-link feedback

**The result**: tens of thousands of kinetic-impact rounds strike
the Others simultaneously. The Others are *marked* — visible dents
appear in their Vessels, faint dimensional-stutter traces. **The
Others do not die.** They consume the Mrokon entirely. But the
SC2-era observation of *"the Others' Vessel has dimples"* — that
canon — is **directly the Hammer-Of-Refusal**. The Mrokon scarred
the apex predator. Permanently.

This is their *Defiant* identity: they will not survive, but they
will *mark*.

## Body Plan

### Operator (sentient)

- **~1m tall**, child-sized, mostly head (large skull housing
  expanded cerebellum and deep-link interface). Body is small,
  spindly, kept indoors and rarely used for surface motion.
- **Skin**: pale near-white, almost translucent (no sun exposure).
- **Eyes**: large dark, optimized for low-light bunker
  environments.
- **Hands**: delicate, designed for puppet-control interface
  manipulation rather than physical labor.
- **Lifespan**: ~120 years.
- **Reproduction**: standard biological birth in shielded nursery-
  bunkers. Young Operators apprentice with elder Operators on
  puppet-control simulations for ~25 years before their first
  field puppet.

### Puppet (combat-body, non-sentient)

- **~2.3m tall**, heavy armored bipedal humanoid.
- **Armor**: layered ceramic-composite over titanium chassis,
  designed to absorb multiple energy / kinetic / explosive hits.
- **Visible scars**: each puppet's chassis is canonically marked
  with the **kill-tallies of its predecessor puppets** (the
  Operator carries their kill-history visibly into each new body).
  Veterans' puppets are heavily scribed; young Operators' first
  puppets are bare.
- **Weapons**: each puppet carries a standard issue **kinetic
  rifle** (medium-damage) plus a single **Hammer-Round** — the
  Operator's reserved final-shot ammunition, kept for the
  Culling event.
- **Deep-link harness**: visible at the back of the puppet's
  neck; a glowing pale-blue interface plate where the Operator's
  neural feed terminates.

## Voice

- **The puppet speaks for the Operator**. Voice is *gruff,
  formal, oath-bound* — the warrior-class voice. Sentences are
  short, declarative, structured around honor and duty.
- **Pronouns**: first-person singular ("I") for the Operator
  *through* the puppet — the Operator and puppet are the same
  "I" for conversational purposes. Mrokon never refer to the
  puppet as a third party.
- **Tone**: solemn but not despairing. The Mrokon know what
  they will do and what it costs; they have decided; they are
  not asking for sympathy.
- **Sample line**:
  > "Steward. I am Vrek-The-Eighth-Body. Three before me have
  > died. I will die. The Hammer is loaded. The Others will
  > be marked. Tell me what you came to ask. I will answer
  > if it does not delay the work."
- **Quirk**: occasionally a puppet *dies mid-conversation* (the
  Operator's previous puppet had taken environmental damage and
  failed; the dialog continues with a *different puppet* arriving
  a few minutes later, picking up exactly where the previous one
  left off, with full memory continuity). This is normal Mrokon
  conversation; visitors learn to tolerate the gap.

## Faction Alignment

**Homesteader (Stay-Side, by combat-doctrine).** They could
migrate (the Furlings have offered transport); they have
refused. *"We will not run. We will not hide. We will mark.
That is what we are for."*

The Mrokon have a quiet, formal respect for the Furlings'
Migration choice. They simply do not share it. They consider
their Hammer-Of-Refusal a *gift* to the survivors — *"The
Others will be marked by us. The marking will help you in
the next galaxy."*

**Terminal: Eliminated** (with full awareness, with the
canonical Marking of the Others as the species' last act).

## In the Slice

**Encounter location**: **Mrokon's Stand** (canonically a rocky
terrestrial world in the slice cluster; the surface is barren
warrior-training grounds; the action is underground in Operator
bunkers and puppet-vaults).

**Encounter shape**: the Steward lands on the surface; a puppet
greets them with formal warrior-protocol; the puppet *might
die* during the initial conversation (training-ground accident,
puppet failure, planned puppet-rotation). A new puppet arrives
within minutes carrying the same Operator's identity. The Steward
adjusts.

**What they want**:
- **Furling engineering help** with the Hammer-Round dimensional-
  resonance tuning. The Mrokon have the kinetic-impact technology;
  the Furlings have the dimensional-substrate analysis. Together
  they can make the Hammer-Round *more effective* at marking.
- **Witness**. They want a Furling Council representative present
  to *see* the Hammer-Of-Refusal fire and to *carry the marking
  to Andromeda*. They want their gesture remembered.

**What we want**:
- **The Hammer-Round dimensional-resonance data** (Hider faction
  archive — invaluable; one of the slice's most valuable archive

  cosmetic slice-epilogue item)

## Ship — The Mrokon Hammer-Ship

A heavy battleship. ~150 points (high for a Homesteader), hull
180 (very tough), shields 60 (medium), slow (top_speed 220),
slow turn (turn_rate 0.6).

- Primary: **Kinetic Cannon** — slow heavy projectile, high
  damage (12 per round), low cadence (0.5 shots/sec). Long
  range (900). The puppet inside is *swapped on the fly* if
  the ship loses one — the Operator just deep-links to a new
  combat-body within the ship.
- Special: **Hammer-Round** — single-use per fight. Fires the
  signature kinetic-impact round. Damage 80 (devastating).
  Speed slow. After firing, the ship is *down* one Operator-
  puppet pair; effective combat capability drops 20% for the
  rest of the fight (the Operator is exhausted from the
  Hammer-Round preparation). Cooldown: never reused in same
  fight.
- AI style: sharpshooter — slow, patient, picks shots. Always
  fires the Hammer-Round when an enemy is within range and the
  shot has good probability.

## Authoring Notes

- **Their primary canonical NPC**: **Vrek-The-Eighth-Body**
  (current puppet). Operator behind the puppet is canonically

  visible damage that no recorded weapon should be able to produce.
  Canonical answer: the Hammer-Of-Refusal. This is now the slice's
  most-significant SC2 canon-forward connection beyond the Taalo
  Shield. **The Mrokon dispatched a permanent message into the
  Others' record; it can be seen in SC2 era; it cannot be undone.**
- **Faction-standing implication**: the Hammer-Round is the only
  slice-event that *physically affects the Others.* All other
  species either survive (Cloaked / Pre-sentient / Hidden /
  Migrated) or are simply consumed (Eliminated). The Mrokon are
  the ONLY species that *touches the predator back.* This is a
  big deal narratively and should be honored in any slice
  epilogue.
- **Visiting the Operator bunker is rare but possible**: only
  after support-the-Hammer is committed and the Mrokon trust the
  Steward, a single Operator (canonically **Vrek**, Vrek-The-
  Eighth-Body's Operator) will permit the Steward into the bunker
  to formally witness the deep-link interface. This is the
  slice's most intimate Mrokon scene.
